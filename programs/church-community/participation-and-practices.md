# Voluntary participation and spiritual practice boundaries

**Primary source:** `NRY-023`, section 1 **Universalist Ethic**, section 2 **Literal vs. Symbolic Interpretation** and **Optional Somatic Practice**, and section 8 **Spiritual Practices Overview**. **Status:** descriptions of a proposed religious community, not evidence of established services.

## Four separate decisions

| Question | Original-source posture | Interpretation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Must someone affirm literal cosmology? | **No.** The package accepts literal and figurative reading of Pleroma, Archons and Aeons | Belief cannot be presumed from attendance |
| Must someone practice martial movement? | **No.** Naijin-Ryū bodily/somatic practice is expressly optional | Physical competence or injury status is not a religious-membership test |
| Must someone share private experiences? | The source encourages voluntary journaling, visualization and group discussion | Invitations to disclose do not create a duty to reveal trauma, intimacy or family history |
| Must someone seek clergy or rank? | Source discusses possible mentorship and recognition routes | A proposed title, Aeon or sash is not automatically a legal, instructional or religious appointment |

## What section 8 actually describes

The source suggests small-scale personal practices using the Aeon Key, reflection and journaling, contemplation, optional reading, mindful movement and gratitude. It also imagines **possible** gatherings such as Shadow or Ascension circles, symbolic ceremonies and guided reflection. These are not confirmed schedules, recognized clinical interventions or currently offered programs.

The Aeon Key has **five modules**; the True Ascension narrative has **six Aeons**; the Book of Signs has **seven signs**; the martial system has **five Sashes of Dawn**. Those are separate taxonomies, not equivalent eligibility ladders.

## Public-safe interpretation

The Church framework can describe an inclusive community vision while keeping private meaning in the hands of each person. Editorial guidance for a future facilitated gathering would include informed consent, privacy, safe exits, no forced visualization or storytelling, and referral to appropriately qualified professionals when clinical support is needed. These are **not claims the source itself has operationalized every safeguard**.

No personal archetype scripts, individual reflections or sensitive case histories from the nested source material are quoted here. The general principle — integration rather than suppression of symbolic parts of self — does not establish a psychiatric theory, an objective inventory of personalities or a diagnosis.
