# Community, clergy and governance — proposal versus authority

**Primary source:** `NRY-023`, section 3 **Governance and Leadership**, section 4 **Clergy and Ordination Model**, section 5 **Articles of Incorporation Language**, and section 6 **Bylaws Outline**. **Evidence class:** historical design only.

## Four roles the source proposes

| Role in the manuscript | Proposed responsibility | What is *not* verified |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Founder / spiritual leader** | Teach the worldview, mentor others, provide continuity of vision and model service rather than dominance | An appointed minister or current legal organizational officer |
| **Emerging clergy / Ascension Guides** | Study, facilitate voluntary discussions and reflective practices, support community learning and be accountable | Ordination, credentials, formal authority, ability to offer therapy or crisis counseling |
| **Board / Church Council** | Administrative and financial oversight, meetings, stewardship, potential conflict-of-interest controls | An existing board, correct bylaws, valid director count, registered organization or approved accounts |
| **Members / community participants** | Optional study, service, gatherings, and possible future organizational membership | Actual enrollment, voting rights, obligations, records or current congregation |

The source describes the clergy primarily as companions and mentors, **not intermediaries standing between people and divinity**. Its code-of-conduct language prioritizes service over power, integrity, compassion, boundaries, self-reflection and accountability.

## Explicit choices still left open by the source

The bylaws outline discusses alternatives rather than making final selections: membership may or may not carry voting rights; directors might be selected by the board or congregation; different officer and council structures are contemplated; future religious ceremonies or leadership titles are not settled. Those draft alternatives **cannot all be promoted into a single, currently binding governance scheme**.

The incorporation section offers suggested legal language and numerical assertions, but no filed instrument or present legal advice. Before any real organizational action, the actual Indiana statutes, governing documents, IRS status and venue-specific obligations would require independent professional or official verification. The source text alone settles none of these.

## Editorial safeguards if a future community were built

These points are **proposals for review**, not clauses known to be adopted:

- **Voluntary participation:** no creed test, forced movement, forced autobiographical disclosure, or loyalty test.
- **Bounded responsibility:** distinguish spiritual discussion facilitators from mental-health professionals, physical instructors and legally authorized officers.
- **Freedom to exit:** participants can decline a practice or leave a group without fear of sanctions for a private belief.
- **Complaint independence:** allegations about a leader must not be assessed solely by the accused leader; external reporting obligations would need actual legal review.
- **Financial and decision transparency:** funding, titles, decisions and conflicts of interest would need documented procedures *if* an institution existed.
- **Private pastoral information:** personal notes and voluntary reflections would require a genuinely controlled storage location rather than public GitHub.

## Hostile review

> **Objection:** Does documenting proposed clergy and a board make it more likely that readers will infer these roles exist, especially when the original is written in a future-present institutional voice?

**Accepted.** This derivative repeatedly labels those roles **historical drafts**, preserves undecided alternatives, and prohibits credential, filing, ordination or operating claims without distinct evidence. A repository page cannot establish the authority it describes.
