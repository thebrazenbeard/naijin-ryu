# Indiana youth camp — legal applicability and readiness evidence

**Scope:** a *proposed* Indiana youth summer camp, **not** an existing registered/inspected camp. **Two independent evidence classes** are used: `SOURCE` refers to the author's historical `NRY-001` planning file; `OFFICIAL_EXTERIOR` refers to the Indiana Department of Health website/rule checked on **2026-10-21**. This is a research matrix, not legal advice, not permission to operate, and not an exhaustive compliance checklist.

## Critical source correction

The historical `Camps Overview` argues that Indiana has no licensing barrier for youth day or overnight camps. **That statement is not a safe planning premise.** The Indiana Department of Health (IDOH) describes a youth-camp program that **inspects camps and approves plans for youth-camp construction or alteration**.

The state's 410 IAC 6-7.2 definition of *youth camp* turns on the actual facts: an area maintained for **more than 72 continuous hours** of outdoor group living away from home; **10 or more children under 18**, without parents/guardians; and educational, recreational, sectarian or health purposes. A “one-week overnight camp” as described in the source *could* satisfy that definition, but capacity, duration, site use and ages require a real project-specific assessment. The manuscript's speculative size of 50 campers is not an observed attendance figure.

Official resources:
- [IDOH Youth Camps program, inspections and permit materials](https://www.in.gov/health/eph/youth-camps/).
- [IDOH 410 IAC 6-7.2 Youth Camps rule](https://www.in.gov/health/eph/laws-rules-and-forms/410-iac-6-72-youth-camps/).
- [Indiana guidance for written youth camp emergency plans](https://www.in.gov/dA/d153da99f2/Guidelines-for-Youth-Camp-Emergency-Plans.pdf?language_id=1).

## What this does not establish

We have **not** determined that any particular camp currently meets the statutory definition, requires a specific license, qualifies for an exception, or has already satisfied state or local authorities. No filing, permit, site inspection, staff screening, insurance, parent permission, medical protocol or financial readiness was observed.

The safe next step before real operation is to define the exact project facts, seek current official/qualified interpretation, and check all applicable programs and local jurisdictions. No registration, construction, recruiting or advertising should be inferred from this research.
