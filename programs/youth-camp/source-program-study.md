# Naijin-Ryū youth summer camp — manuscript-specific concept study

**Primary sources:** `NRY-001`, *Camps Overview.docx*, and `NRY-002`, its PDF edition. The source describes **two distinct proposed camps** in one planning file. **Only** the Naijin-Ryū children's summer camp belongs to this workstream. The separate fall/winter horror concept does not belong to the martial school or Church merely because it shares a manuscript.

## What the summer camp source actually proposes

The overview introduces a **seasonal overnight youth martial arts camp**, provisionally sized at **up to 50 participants**, with an example focus on children around **8–12 years old**. These are *assumptions in a plan*, not an enrollment cap approved by a regulator or a verified demand forecast.

The youth camp portions identify activities oriented around Naijin-Ryū principles: welcome/orientation, movement and stance study, ethics, meditation or journaling as optional reflection, basic supervised skills, noncompetitive group games, physical conditioning, evening discussion, rest and eventual recognition. The document includes a **one-week sample schedule** — an initial arrival/orientation day, recurring days **2–6**, and a final-day departure concept — rather than a fully staffed operational timetable.

The same source also outlines staffing, overnight supervision, food service, sanitation, emergency response, health documentation, budget and insurance ideas. Its numerical staffing ratios, legal statements, expense projections and specific physical-activity choices must be treated as **historical planning assumptions**, not binding rules or medically cleared practice.

## Position within Naijin-Ryū

The camp can introduce **Guard Life, Not Ego** and basic developmental movement, but camp attendance is not a sash award, teacher qualification or proof of defensive skill. Spiritual reflection should not demand literal acceptance of True Ascension or disclosure of private experiences. Actual participation would require parental/guardian processes when appropriate and age/ability accommodations.

The source also proposes sparring and grappling. This public document **does not authorize contact training**. Before any actual youth instruction, competent supervision, child safeguarding, age-appropriate exercises, venue controls and individualized consent need verification.

## Editorial warning about camp scope

Indiana's official youth-camp rule applies only if a proposed site satisfies its defined facts; even a proposal that falls outside that definition can face other health, building, food, child-safety or local rules. The manuscript's general “no state licensing” statement is **not an adequate compliance conclusion**. See [state applicability and evidence matrix](indiana-readiness-matrix.md).
