# One-week youth camp — optional noncontact educational concept

**SOURCE:** `NRY-001`, *Camps Overview*, “Martial Arts Camp (kids)” and “Sample Daily Schedule (Template)”. The author’s plan includes a one-week example with group orientation, movement instruction, games, meals, reflection and departure. **EDITORIAL:** the accessible, noncontact topic schedule below deliberately replaces the source's contact drills, sparring and unsupervised environmental challenges. This is **not** an approved day camp or physical-instruction syllabus.

## Educational topics, not a daily operating timetable

| Concept day | Source theme or inspiration | Proposed lower-risk learning objective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Arrival / orientation** | Group welcome, rules, philosophy | Explain “Guard Life, Not Ego,” personal boundaries, instructor help and the right to stop |
| **Stability** | Stance, balance, basic movement | Noncontact balance games suited to age, ability and professional review |
| **Awareness** | Environment, attention and group drills | Identify safe pathways, exits, weather signs and reasons to ask for assistance |
| **Restraint** | Ethical conduct and partner respect | Practice words for de-escalation, consent, respecting others' space and seeking staff help |
| **Shared learning** | Forms, team games and reflection | Cooperative movement challenges without contact, competition pressure or compulsory spiritual belief |
| **Consolidation** | Repeating instruction and character discussion | Learners describe what they tried, how they stopped safely and when to seek an adult |
| **Departure** | Source proposes recognition and checkout | Acknowledge participation without awarding sashes, combat certifications or spiritual status |

The topics **do not correspond exactly** to each clock entry in the author's sample; no staffing, duration, lunch, medication, transport or sleep schedule is authorized here. Before such a schedule exists, legal applicability, child welfare practices, food and lodging, guardian communication, staff competencies and site safety need independent design and review.

## Child autonomy and spiritual optionality

No child needs to confess intimate experiences or accept the Six Aeons as literal fact. Reflective activities should provide secular options and a choice not to disclose. The Church package explicitly permits literal or symbolic interpretation and treats martial practice as optional in that religious context; this youth camp concept cannot reverse those commitments.

A child should never be pressured to demonstrate a physical technique to earn acceptance. Keep blank plans in public Git and individual child records only in an authorized, appropriately protected real system if one ever exists.

## Non-operational completion criterion

This public lesson concept can be **editorially complete** while the camp itself remains **not launched**. Staff, medical capacity, youth safety, insurance, legal requirements, facility approvals, privacy, guardian permissions, and program authority remain unverified. The reviewer must not convert a published timetable into permission to receive children.
