# Naijin-Ryū Phase 1 — eight-week guided solo course

**Coursebook status:** **EDITORIAL SOLO INTRODUCTION — NOT AN AUTHOR-APPROVED CONTACT SYLLABUS OR CERTIFICATION.** Source `NRY-013` supplies an **eight-week, four-day Phase 1** and the four day names: **Shock, Unbalance, Reversal, Grapple**. **Environment** is observed each day; its dedicated day appears in Phase 2. Source `NRY-012` separately contains a **six-week individualized Fundamentals** edition; `NRY-007` also includes a longer Foundation lattice. This eight-week set does not silently overwrite those original variants.

## Start here

1. Choose the current week based on completed, comfortable work **not calendar elapsed time**. No session has to happen on a named weekday. If no actual baseline is recorded, begin with [Week 1](phase-1-restart-week.md).
2. Open one day's session below. Read the instructions and choose the lower-impact alternative before beginning. Clear the floor and do the check-in described in [Session A](phase-1-session-a.md).
3. Practice at a sustainable, conversational effort. **A solo plan cannot qualify strikes, throws, joint locks, breakfalls or submissions.** Do not replicate the contact procedures from the source without appropriately qualified supervision.
4. Afterward, write only in a **private** copy of the [blank log](restart-session-log.md): what you actually did, changes made, effort, unexpected symptoms, and later/next-day recovery.
5. Use the [progression gates](phase-1-progression-gates.md) to choose **repeat, reduce, recover or carefully vary** the next session. No self-awarded sash or mandatory spiritual exercises.

## Open a week

| Week | Work focus | Four complete session cards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [1](phase-1-restart-week.md) | **Baseline / return to training** | Four introductory sessions A–D; posture, self-stability, control and safe disengagement. |
| [2](phase-1-week-02.md) | **Repeatable base** | Repeat familiar steps slowly, with deliberate resets. |
| [3](phase-1-week-03.md) | **Direction variety** | Use small squares and clock-face steps; identify clear routes. |
| [4](phase-1-week-04.md) | **Consolidation** | Simpler checkpoint; evaluate comfort, balance, stop rules. |
| [5](phase-1-week-05.md) | **Linking concepts** | Combine one controlled action with another without contact or rush. |
| [6](phase-1-week-06.md) | **Benign changes** | Re-select clear routes after a calm stop/change cue. |
| [7](phase-1-week-07.md) | **Repeatability** | Choose two familiar drills, compare control without testing power. |
| [8](phase-1-week-08.md) | **Review, not automatic graduation** | Revisit Week 1 baseline; identify what only a qualified human can assess. |

For **Weeks 2–8**, each week's page includes its own **A, B, C and D** clock tables. Each week continues the same optional time budgets (A 30, B 25, C 26, D 24 minutes) while **the learning task changes**. This is deliberate: repeatable duration helps judge movement quality without automatically escalating volume. **105 minutes/week is a maximum suggested four-session package**, not a quota, not necessarily all aerobic activity, and not suitable for everyone. A session can be shortened or replaced by [low-impact cardio](cardio-base-track.md) when appropriate.

## What each day practices

| Day | Source pillar | Solo editorial analogue | NOT tested by the analogue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Shock | Comfortable guard, controlled straight-line or lateral footwork, optional air-hand movement | Striking power, incapacitation or contact timing |
| **B** | Unbalance | Recover one's own base, supported shifts, posture | Ability to throw, trip, or unbalance an opponent |
| **C** | Reversal | Stop, pivot gently, notice exit and change direction | Ability to counter a grab, punch or joint lock |
| **D** | Grapple | Personal space, stance, stepping away and respecting 'stop' | Clinch, breakfalls, ground submissions or physical control |
| **All** | Environment | Hazards, bystanders, clear routes and avoidance | Threat-response effectiveness |

## Training adaptation is the rule, not the exception

**Stop the relevant activity** if unexpected pain, weakness, dizziness, unstable footing or other concerning symptoms arise; do not interpret clearance for general exercise as clearance for an unfamiliar movement. Pace, duration and whether punches are appropriate are individual decisions. Reflection and spiritual claims are optional, never proof of qualification.

**Source limitations:** Source `NRY-013` actually describes contact drills, whereas these cards intentionally use noncontact introductory analogues. They are an **accessible entry path**, not the whole martial art. Advancing toward the original contact syllabus would require an instructor to show safe control, consent, protective environment, relevant competencies and a planned escalation method.

## Instructor / coach handoff

A qualified instructor reviewing a student should use [the observation protocol](phase-1-instructor-observation.md) to distinguish **solo-reported** from **personally observed**, **assessed** and **authorized**. There is no invented automatic numerical threshold for rank or real defensive ability. An instructor may keep a student at this foundation or build a separate supervised-contact module only with appropriate controls.
