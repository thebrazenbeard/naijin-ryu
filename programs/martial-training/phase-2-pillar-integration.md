# Phase 2 — Pillar Integration (working weeks 9–16)

**Source scope:** `NRY-013`, *Phases of SURGE Training Manual.docx*, Part I, heading “Phase 2 – Pillar Integration (Weeks 9–16, 5 days/week)”, weekly structure table and “Phase 2 Summary – Key Developments.” Cross-reference: `NRY-007`, main martial manual §IV. **Status:** source-grounded overview with an explicitly **editorial, non-operational learning plan**.

## What the manuscript actually establishes

The source moves from Phase 1's isolated introduction to each principle toward **connections between SURGE pillars**. Its example training week assigns one theme to each of **Shock, Unbalance, Reversal, Grapple and Environment**. The fifth Environment day is newly dedicated here; Phase 1 instead included Environment mostly through awareness. The original contains combative combinations, joint-lock and takedown sequences, constrained-space confrontation simulations and light sparring.

The text proposes **five practice days a week**. This is a manuscript timetable, not proof that five sessions are medically appropriate for every student, that any drill is legally justified, or that a skill works under actual attack. This public derivative does not repeat injurious sequences or claim a sixteenth-week graduate has achieved combat readiness.

## Editorial learning sequence (not a verbatim syllabus)

| Working weeks | Development theme | Appropriate lower-risk learning evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **9–10** | Re-establish Phase 1 competencies before combining concepts; introduce Environment as a named daily theme | Student distinguishes all five pillars, identifies exits/hazards, communicates pause/stop clearly |
| **11–12** | Connect posture, timing, awareness and choice between two pillar themes **without mandatory impact or resistance** | Instructor observes transitions between noncontact movement tasks and an unpressured decision to disengage |
| **13–14** | Adapt to variation and a partner's feedback while preserving consent and individual limits | Student notices loss of balance, corrects safely, changes task or asks to stop before control deteriorates |
| **15–16** | Consolidate cross-pillar understanding, recovery and ethical decisions | Student explains why force is not required in a scenario and demonstrates safe participation in instructor-selected tasks |

The eight-week grouping and evidence descriptions above are **editorial proposals** distilled from the source's phase objectives. The manuscript does **not** list these exact two-week modules or prescribe these assessments. They require qualified human review before physical instruction.

## Five named day themes and distinct responsibilities

| Source day | Source topic | Public course emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — Shock** | Striking combinations | Movement organization, spatial control, restraint, and awareness of unintended impact; no public strike-sequence instructions |
| **2 — Unbalance** | Throws and setups | Maintaining one's own stability and understanding balance/weight shift in non-injurious demonstrations |
| **3 — Reversal** | Locks and transitions | Controlled direction changes, noticing resistance, and **immediate release on a stop signal** |
| **4 — Grapple** | Takedown to control | Position and personal-space vocabulary, fall-prevention and consent; no unsupervised submissions or takedowns |
| **5 — Environment** | Scenario training | Recognizing bystanders, doors, surfaces, obstacles, escape opportunities, and when to leave |

The source's techniques are preserved in the private original. These public summaries intentionally favor **judgment and safe movement** over a replicable harmful-combat drill catalogue.

## Review gates before advancing

**Source-derived direction:** integration should remain adaptive and respectful of ethical restraint. **Editorial safeguarding gate:** a reviewer should observe that the student can (1) honor stop signals immediately; (2) adapt intensity to partner ability, disability, injury and environment; (3) recover balance or disengage rather than escalating; (4) identify a nonphysical alternative; and (5) accurately state when help, recovery or further instruction is needed.

An assessor can record **observed**, **developing**, **not yet demonstrated**, or **not assessed**. The last category is crucial: missing evidence is not failure, and a calendar date is not qualification. Named student/medical notes belong in private systems, not this public repo.

## Claims the source does not independently prove

The source's assertions about the reliability of strikes, transitions, biomechanical effects and functioning under multiple threats have not been independently qualified here. The transition to contact drills, if ever planned, requires competent supervision, appropriate safety controls and individualized consent. Spiritual participation is not a prerequisite.
