# Phase 1 — Session B: Unbalance, self-stability and recovery

**Source:** `NRY-013`, Phase 1 “Day 2: Unbalance Training – Off-Balancing Fundamentals.” The original teaches opponent kuzushi, trips and throws; **this public solo restart intentionally does not teach or rehearse those contact techniques**. It focuses on understanding *your own* base and safe changes of stance. **All timings, movements and assessments below are EDITORIAL.**

**Target:** approximately **25 minutes**, solo, noncontact; equipment optional: stable chair or wall for balance support. Avoid slippery surfaces and clear obstacles before starting.

| Time | Segment | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0–5 min** | Easy preparation | Walk or march lightly, then practice comfortable ankle and hip mobility. Keep your shoulders relaxed. |
| **5–12 min** | Base and stance transitions | Practice a comfortable stance and step forward/back, side-to-side, and back to the starting base without crossing into your own feet. Slow down whenever posture or footing becomes uncertain. |
| **12–17 min** | Supported balance | With a stable support within reach, shift weight left/right; optionally take brief single-leg balance attempts if appropriate. **No forced prolonged holds**, instability or partner manipulation. |
| **17–22 min** | Aerobic recovery | Alternate a comfortable brisk walk with easier walking according to breathing and how stable you feel. Both hands may remain relaxed; punching is unnecessary. |
| **22–25 min** | Reset and private note | Walk slowly, observe footing and recovery, and note one thing that affected balance. |

## Observable practice criteria

A useful session demonstrates controlled self-positioning, an ability to stop when balance deteriorates, and a willingness to use a support rather than pushing through instability. These are **practice observations**, not proficiency in throwing another person.

**If balance or movement causes discomfort:** shorten the range, use support, replace the task with walking, or stop. If symptoms persist or are new and concerning, obtain qualified guidance. A previous injury clearance is not authorization for every specific exercise.

**Optional mental theme:** `NRY-009` Day 2 connects Unbalance with emotional centering. As an editorial reflection, ask: *What helped me regain my center without rushing?* No physiological or therapeutic effect is implied.
