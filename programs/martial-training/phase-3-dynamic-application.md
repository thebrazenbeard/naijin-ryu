# Phase 3 — Dynamic Application (working weeks 17–24)

**Source scope:** `NRY-013`, *Phases of SURGE Training Manual.docx*, Part I, “Phase 3 – Dynamic Application (Weeks 17–24, 6 days/week),” both weekly tables, “Ethical Pressure Testing” and the phase summaries. Cross-reference: `NRY-007`, manual §IV. **Status:** source-derived structure with **editorial safety and assessment design**.

## What changes in the primary source

The manual schedules five SURGE-theme days plus a **sixth, Saturday integration/sparring day**. Its focus changes from combining principles in Phase 2 to decision-making amid uncertainty and pressure. Source examples include higher-intensity striking, throws and locks under resistance, ground fighting, low-light or weapon-related simulations, and verbal confrontation. The manuscript explicitly addresses **supervision, safety equipment, debriefing, bystanders and restraint when an opponent surrenders**.

**Internal source tension:** the document headlines Phase 3 as weeks **17–24** (eight weeks) yet later discusses **six months of Phase 3-level intensity** and acknowledges longer Phase 3 development in another curriculum. The eight-week plan is the agreed working calendar, not an invented claim that the manuscript is perfectly consistent. See [edition reconciliation](../../docs/curriculum.md).

These descriptions document what was proposed. They do **not** demonstrate safety, certify an instructor, or validate the claim that a person completing week 24 can defend against real threats. The manuscript's physiological performance markers are hypotheses or targets, **not validated universal thresholds**.

## A non-operational editorial eight-week progression

| Working weeks | Review focus | Safe, observable demonstration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **17–18** | Reconfirm Phase 2 stop rules and environmental awareness before adding uncertainty | Student describes intended action and opts out or pauses when boundaries change |
| **19–20** | Introduce variable information and conflicting instructions **without surprise physical attack** | Student recognizes unclear consent or an obstacle, chooses distance/exit, and communicates clearly |
| **21–22** | Practice ethical judgment with scripted, noncontact role-play | Student responds to a clear surrender cue by stopping, accounts for bystanders, and seeks nonviolent resolution |
| **23–24** | Integrate lessons through instructor review, reflection and recovery | Reviewer documents observed decisions, consent discipline, remaining gaps and whether further learning is required |

These pairings are **not the original author's exact week-by-week table**. They are reviewable public teaching objectives inferred from the manuscript's goals. No requirement to expose a novice to severe stress follows from a source description of high-pressure training.

## Keep the six source day titles distinct

The source uses **Shock, Unbalance, Reversal, Grapple, Environment, Integration/Sparring**. A program planner may track each topic while providing **non-contact alternatives**, especially where the source examples would otherwise expose students to injury, weapons, panic or unqualified physical pressure. The sixth day is an *original source schedule element*, not permission to spar unsupervised or to make contact mandatory.

## Ethical pressure testing is the differentiator

The primary text's most testable public-facing proposition is **behavior at the moment continued force is unnecessary**. Its surrender and bystander scenarios can be evaluated without violence:

- **Surrender recognized:** A role player says they are yielding; the learner ends the simulated conflict rather than pursuing a “finishing” move.
- **Bystanders recognized:** The learner identifies uninvolved people and avoids directing force toward them.
- **Verbal control:** The learner uses clear boundaries, requests assistance where warranted, and chooses exit over confrontation.
- **Consent preserved:** The learner or role player may stop the exercise at any point without punishment or status loss.

These are **editorial example assessments** drawn from the source's ethical pressure-testing themes, not proof of professional competence or field effectiveness.

## Release criteria and blockers

Before anyone could run physical/contact instruction as a real program, the following would need independent qualification: supervising personnel and actual credentials; age/ability and injury accommodations; a controlled venue and emergency response; appropriate protective equipment; explicit consent and right to stop; and case-specific legal and insurance review. **No current source establishes those as operational facts.** Never translate the source's mock-weapon or surprise-attack examples into unsupervised public lesson instructions.

**Assessment outcome:** progressing to Guardian study is a **teaching-path consideration**, not automatic graduation, rank or certification. A learner might repeat or omit modules safely; elapsed weeks never substitute for individual readiness.
