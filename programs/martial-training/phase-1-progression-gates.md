# Phase 1 — return-to-practice progression and review gates

**Original source:** `NRY-013` provides Phase 1 **eight weeks, four pillar-themed days/week** and explicitly allows recovery and review. `NRY-012` retains a separate six-week Fundamentals version; `NRY-007` includes an extended Foundation lattice. Those are genuine original variants, **not errors to silently erase**.

**EDITORIAL:** This page establishes a practical first-week decision system, re-entry alternatives, and a course-review framework. It does **not** specify medical rehabilitation, contact competency, rank, competition clearance, spiritual advancement or instructor authority.

## Do not treat four sessions as automatic readiness

| What was observed | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| Controlled movement, chosen effort manageable, and usual later/next-day recovery | **Option:** repeat the session or modestly extend only **one** dimension (duration *or* complexity), leaving other variables stable |
| Mixed coordination, inconsistent pace or uncertainty about a movement | **Repeat / simplify:** lower task complexity and practice at an easier pace |
| Excessive fatigue or increased discomfort | **Reduce / recover:** shorten or omit the relevant work and reconsider recovery needs |
| Sharp pain, unexpected weakness, joint instability, dizziness or otherwise concerning symptoms | **Stop relevant activity / seek qualified assessment** rather than self-prescribing a harder variation |
| No practice data yet | **UNKNOWN:** no inferred progress, rank, capacity or need to escalate |

These dispositions are **editorial decision rules** and must be applied with current individual circumstances and any relevant clinician/instructor advice. The participant can decline any movement or reflection without being judged as noncompliant.

## Weeks 1–8: editorial decision gates aligned to source themes

| Working phase | Proposed content | What to review (not what to certify) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Week 1 — baseline** | [A](phase-1-session-a.md), [B](phase-1-session-b.md), [C](phase-1-session-c.md), [D](phase-1-session-d.md), with recovery as needed | Comfortable duration, control, environmental awareness, symptom response |
| **Week 2 — repeat or stabilize** | Repeat the four day themes or shorten them if needed | Whether more consistent self-positioning and recovery are observed |
| **Weeks 3–4 — movement variation** | Introduce a little variety to noncontact step directions and attention tasks *only after the earlier baseline remains comfortable* | Preservation of balance and the ability to stop |
| **Weeks 5–6 — conceptual integration** | Recall each pillar and combine basic stance, footwork, awareness and refusal/de-escalation in harmless scenarios | Can distinguish **Shock, Unbalance, Reversal, Grapple, Environment** without performing injurious techniques |
| **Weeks 7–8 — consolidation and review** | Repeat manageable drills, debrief and assess ethical choices; additional rest if warranted | An authorized instructor could review observed basics; **no automatic rank, advancement or self-defense qualification** |

The table's specific grouping is **EDITORIAL**, building on [the existing eight-week framework](../../docs/training/8-week-foundation.md). It is not a direct transcription of the original's drills or biomedical targets. If a learner is not ready, the appropriate response may be to extend a week, change a task, or remain at Phase 1.

## Detailed teaching route

Open the [complete eight-week course](phase-1-eight-week-course.md) for links to the **32 specific individual sessions**. Weeks 2–8 retain the Week 1 time budgets but vary the learning tasks rather than escalating physical load on a calendar. ## Cardio and recovery companion

The public restart sessions include brief easy-to-brisk walking or marching. For intensity, the current CDC [talk test](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html) provides an *external* check: moderate-intensity aerobic activity generally allows speaking but not singing. The [federal physical activity guidance](https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/physical-activity-guidelines/about-physical-activity-guidelines/questions-answers) recommends starting with short amounts and building gradually for previously inactive adults.

**EXTERNAL ≠ SOURCE:** Neither guidance establishes the safety or efficacy of a specific Naijin-Ryū martial exercise. The session times are **starter proposals**, not the national 150-minute target, a cardiovascular fitness test, or proof of medical clearance.

If a participant wishes to develop longer cardio work, additional *comfortable* walking may be added on a separate day or after a review; do not raise multiple workloads at once. Any medical limitations are individual and belong in private consultation, not a public source register.

## Traceability without private health records

Use the [blank progress log](restart-session-log.md) in a private location. Preserve the difference between **scheduled**, **performed**, **observed**, **assessed by an authorized instructor**, and **qualified by an authorized decision maker**. Missing measurements are UNKNOWN, not evidence of low skill. Do not put identifiable recovery logs in public branches.
