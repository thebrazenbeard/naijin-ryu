# Naijin-Ryū V2 — Student Phases, actual training architecture

**Status:** PROPOSED EDITORIAL TEACHING REVISION; **not** a replacement for or verbatim reproduction of NRY-007 / NRY-010 / NRY-012 / NRY-013. Read the [martial rewrite](NAIJIN_RYU_MARTIAL_MANUAL_V2.md) and [original schedule index](../original-training-schedules.md) first. This document intentionally uses the original **four / five / six days per week** progression and source-specific technical families. Class plans, tasks and gates below are newly authored and need qualified physical-teaching review. No school, qualified instructor or safe partner setup is assumed to exist.

## What is retained from the source, and what is changed

**Source-preserved:** Phase 1 Fundamentals Activation (weeks 1–8, 4 pillar days); Phase 2 Pillar Integration (weeks 9–16, 5 pillar days including dedicated Environment); Phase 3 Dynamic Application (weeks 17–24, six days including an integration/sparring theme); Phase 4 Guardian's Path. Four primary early pillars S/U/R/G and E awareness; source technical families include palm-heel / elbow concepts, off-balancing/kuzushi, redirection/pivots, clinch/ground concepts and adaptive environmental attention. The individualized NRY-010 also specifies daily **Morning Forge (~30 minutes)**, main session **approximately 60–90 minutes** in Phase 1, Evening Stillness and recovery days. Other source editions differ and are retained.

**Rewritten:** A day is organized around a *task and evidence*, not a promise of neurological incapacitation or a choreographed five-pillar response. Risk is gated by the actual coach, space and student. A four-day Phase 1 week need not mandate five mental-conditioning sessions; NRY-009 remains a separate optional five-theme source.

## 1. Class framework (default teaching template, not source timing)

A proposed adult coached session has six parts:

| Part | Phase 1 suggested time | Content |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Pre-brief | 5 min | Purpose; check floor, equipment, consent, individual limits, exit and stop word |
| Prepare | 10 min | Warm-up through comfortable movement and mobility; staged intensities |
| Technical task | 20 min | Source-aligned S/U/R/G family; demonstrate, coached practice, correct error |
| Constraint / decision | 10 min | A permitted low-risk change of pace, direction or cue; record if control persists |
| Conditioning | 10 min | Appropriately dosed movement, balance and aerobic work; do not call every minute cardio |
| Cooldown / debrief | 5 min | Recover; one observation, one failure/unknown, one next decision |

**60 minutes is an EDITORIAL teaching example**, consistent with the order of magnitude of the six-week NRY-012 source but not an authorized medical workload. A founder following NRY-010's **60–90-minute** main session may extend practice in appropriate parts; do not automatically stack Morning Forge onto an already excessive workload. All segments are adjustable by readiness and trained supervision.

**A second, optional framing:** a secular student can skip HaraBreath ritual, mantras, Triad Journal and Aeonic reflection; short attention and task-based debrief still occur as ordinary coaching. Spiritual participation may be individually meaningful but must not determine martial assessment.

## 2. Daily pillar sessions: the technical identity must remain visible

### S — Shock day: defensive initiative

- **Foundation:** stance and distance; hand organization; paced controlled reach/return; short footwork; awareness of when *not* to strike.
- **Source-aligned technical family:** palm-heel and elbow fundamentals, taught with appropriate legal, medical and contact-risk considerations, never as guaranteed concussion-free stuns. At novice level, teach *alignment and air or suitably supervised pad contact*, not dangerous targeting.
- **Allowed low-risk cue:** one clearly disclosed color or verbal cue requires a step and guard reset rather than a strike; score balanced recovery and stopping.
- **Sample observation:** can maintain a protective posture, avoid overreach and stop on command; do not score “brainstem disruption.”

### U — Unbalance day: base management

- **Foundation:** base width, center-of-mass shift, safe support and recovery, posture while another person changes position.
- **Source-aligned technical family:** kuzushi, push/pull sensitivity, trips and throws as *supervised future techniques*; no partner sweeping without safe ukemi training, appropriately skilled coach and mats.
- **Allowed low-risk cue:** a cooperative partner changes **their own** foot placement while the student observes/adjusts their stance without pulling, tripping or twisting.
- **Sample observation:** regains own base consistently and respects both people's space; no claim that every partner can be unbalanced regardless of size.

### R — Reversal day: perceive and re-route

- **Foundation:** tenkan-inspired pivots and angle shifts, distance management, body/foot synchronization and immediate disengagement.
- **Source-aligned technical family:** wrist-control/joint-lock concepts remain in the historical curriculum but do not automatically move into unsupervised force application.
- **Allowed low-risk cue:** trainer signals a changed walking line; student pauses, checks a clear path and turns without contact.
- **Sample observation:** chooses a safe alternative rather than finishing the first planned movement at any cost.

### G — Grapple day: close range and safe separation

- **Foundation:** safe positioning, verbal boundaries, low stance, balance and return to standing. With coach/mat review, proper ukemi and constrained partner positioning can later be introduced.
- **Source-aligned technical family:** clinch, takedown concepts, positional control and ground literacy. **No neck compression training, dangerous pins or forced joint loading from this document.**
- **Allowed low-risk cue:** start at a safe distance, step to create space, honor “stop” immediately and identify the exit.
- **Sample observation:** learns positional vocabulary and safe separation. Solitary demonstration cannot verify live grappling performance.

### E — Environment: every day, dedicated from Phase 2

- Scan before setting a drill; read space, surface, bystanders and multiple-person risk. Respect the impossibility of controlling every uncertainty.
- Sample cue: “your chosen doorway is now blocked”—student stops, chooses a clear alternative; no surprise weapon/simulated ambush or increased physical risk.
- Record whether the student actually changed the plan rather than describing a preferred heroic outcome.

## 3. Phase 1 — Fundamentals Activation (weeks 1–8)

**Original rhythm:** S/U/R/G on four separate days; recovery/review otherwise, Environment throughout. **V2 objective:** establish a *technical base worth teaching* without treating a solo substitute as equivalent to the original contact syllabus. Original NRY-012's six-week option and NRY-007's extended variant remain source documents, not erased.

| Week | S: Shock | U: Unbalance | R: Reversal | G: Grapple | E + phase question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — identify** | Neutral guard and safe return | Observe own balance | Stop/side-step/turn | Personal space, posture | Can the learner state **when not to engage**? |
| **2 — repeat** | Guard and pace, left/right comfortable air mechanics | Recover after small weight shift | Reproduce gentle pivots | Low vs upright stance literacy | Can the learner stop and reset without hurry? |
| **3 — timing** | Short step then controlled technique silhouette | Step and stabilize | Turn after a cue | Approach/retreat distance without contact | Was a route chosen before moving? |
| **4 — recover** | Clear error review; decrease load if needed | Stable base with rested conditions | Choose a simpler alternative | Falling-safety **concepts**, not unpadded ukemi | Is discomfort or fatigue recognized early? |
| **5 — connect** | Footwork plus guarded safe air motion | Base adjustment before task | Pivot then deliberate separation | Separate from close position (noncontact) | Can two decisions be linked **without forced sequence**? |
| **6 — vary** | One change of starting distance | One change of safe direction | One changed verbal cue | One changed but benign space arrangement | Does control persist across a small variation? |
| **7 — revisit** | Repeat earlier task and check guard | Compare two base shifts | Explain one abandoned option | Demonstrate immediate release/stop response | Are failures named rather than concealed? |
| **8 — review** | Coached technical observation; may stay noncontact | Movement / balance review | Adaptive choice review | Positional boundaries review | What is **not yet tested** by this phase? |

**End gate:** can demonstrate controlled fundamental movement, identify safety limits, communicate consent and correctly distinguish the pillars. Readiness for throws, locks, strikes under pressure, ukemi or submissions remains **unverified until separately assessed in appropriate conditions**. Delaying progression is neither failure nor spiritual deficiency.

**Solo founder use:** NRY-010 Morning Forge and Evening Stillness may continue as personal practice. Its postoperative milestones were time-specific historical instructions, not a current medical clearance or forecast. Any current exercises or physical capacities require personal observation and qualified support as relevant.

## 4. Phase 2 — Pillar Integration (weeks 9–16)

**Original rhythm:** five pillar days S/U/R/G/E with Environment its own study. **V2 objective:** choose among pillars, including doing less, with safe, clearly constrained partner work when qualified instructors and facilities exist.

| Week | Major integration question | S / U / R / G technical agenda | Dedicated E task | Evidence to gather |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **9** | Can the same cue permit *more than one* safe choice? | Review foundations under new start postures | Two exits, one blocked | Records choice and rationale |
| **10** | Does movement retain balance when tasks change? | Guard, base, pivot, separation in distinct controlled stations | Different clear floor route | Notes loss and recovery of balance |
| **11** | Can distance be managed without urgent contact? | Coached range observation; pad or contact only with safeguards | Bystander positions represented by safe markers | Does not cross protected bystander space |
| **12** | What happens when a rehearsed action fails? | Coach announces benign task failure; learner stops and resets | Rerouted evacuation/exit | Can abandon script immediately |
| **13** | Can cooperative partner feedback improve understanding? | Opt-in coach-approved partner positioning; no neck/impact risk | Identify supervision and equipment hazards | Safe release / stop behavior |
| **14** | Is the pillar label useful or distracting? | Present similar task with two plausible classifications | Compare one low-risk alternative | Justifies choice from result, not vocabulary |
| **15** | Can tasks be recalled after a pause? | Review technical principles on different days | Route choice after break | Repeatability / retention |
| **16** | What is actually ready for more variation? | Separately evaluate tasks with controlled exposure | End-to-end hazard audit | Scope-specific documented decision |

**Contact gating:** the author-prescribed Phase 2 inclusion of partner feedback and control work is retained as a goal, **not** automatically authorized for anyone holding Week 9. If the necessary qualified instructors, mats, consent or incident response aren't available, teach the noncontact portion and **record the rest as not delivered**.

## 5. Phase 3 — Dynamic Application (weeks 17–24)

**Original rhythm:** six days, five pillars plus a supervised Saturday integration/sparring theme. **V2 objective:** demonstrate adaptive, ethically bounded action against **approved, predictable risk** and only such physical variability as qualified supervisors deem acceptable.

| Week | Pillar day emphasis | Sixth-day integration alternative | What would falsify a claim of progress? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **17** | Reinforce S/U/R/G/E and floor/supervision rules | Noncontact multi-choice route game | Learner rushes through a blocked route |
| **18** | Maintain control while movement tempo changes safely | Coach-led benign cue sequencing | Requires constant scripted repetition |
| **19** | Distinguish contact decision from disengagement | Cooperative scenario / no attack simulation required | Defaults to force when exit exists |
| **20** | Recover after intentional low-risk task interruption | Pauses, restarts, consent rehearsal | Ignores stop or fatigue signal |
| **21** | Integrate stance transitions and awareness | Approved partner positional station if qualified; otherwise no contact | Loses balance with minor new constraint |
| **22** | Compare two teaching approaches | Safe task taught both as demonstration and varied problem | No robust difference despite complexity added |
| **23** | Retention and decision quality | Mixed non-injurious stations and recovery | Cannot explain what remains untested |
| **24** | Review and instructor readiness limits | Independent observer assesses the **declared** training tasks | Ceremony or grading replaces actual observation |

**Sparring:** The original names a sixth integration/sparring day; the v2 does not claim ordinary reading, soft pads or training hours qualify someone for live sparring. Only qualified operators can design restricted contact practice; a noncontact substitute is not a sparring result. No head-strike, neck-restraint, dangerous throw, live weapon or “ambush” procedures are supplied or authorized here.

## 6. Phase 4 — Guardian's Path

This is a **continuing teaching/apprenticeship and self-correction path**, not a terminal mastery certificate.

- **Technical stewardship:** maintain and assess task-specific skill, acknowledge weaker areas and seek outside coaches in relevant martial disciplines.
- **Safety stewardship:** supervise boundaries, consent, facilities and emergency plans within actual competency, including reporting mistakes.
- **Pedagogy:** explain the task, show its conditions, adapt to a learner's needs, assess fairly and teach appropriate limitations.
- **Ethical stewardship:** reject humiliation, coercive spiritual demands, unauthorized risky experiments and pressure to fight.
- **Institutional humility:** allow outside critique and appeal. A person cannot self-award teaching authority by writing the grading standard.

## 7. Technique-training decision levels

| Level | Conditions | What can be done / claimed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0 — Concepts** | Reading or discussion | Can discuss origins and ethics; cannot claim movement competence |
| **1 — Solo** | Clear safe floor; acceptable symptoms | Controlled stance, guard, travel, air movement and decision rehearsals; no partner competence claimed |
| **2 — Cooperative supervised** | Qualified coach, safe partner agreement, appropriate equipment | Limited body-position tasks and instructor-selected fundamentals; no resistance competence claimed |
| **3 — Constrained variation** | Verified Level 2, safeguards, bounded feedback | Small explicitly permitted variation; count failures/stops and adverse events |
| **4 — Representative contact** | Independent professional risk evaluation, suitable venue/consent and qualified oversight | Only task-specific conclusions from accurately documented practice; **not** blanket self-defense validation |
| **Unreviewed / restricted** | High-risk contact or no competent oversight | Not part of this public curriculum |

**Levels are proposed editorial training categories**, *not* automatically awarded ranks or a substitute for the actual safety rules of qualified martial arts organizations. Any pressure test beyond current safe capacity is deferred.

## 9. A truly adversarial course review

> **Critic:** Does this twenty-four-week matrix make the art look more comprehensive than the participants' actual contact skills?

**Accepted.** Each week is a **planning cell**, not completed training. Failure to provide qualified contact instruction leaves genuine source martial content **uncovered**, even when all conceptual alternatives were completed. No instructor should misrepresent this revised curriculum as “pressure tested” until real bounded tests occur.

> **Critic:** Does rigidly preserving six days in Phase 3 ignore evidence of fatigue and personal recovery?

**Partially accepted.** Six is the source's **schedule theme**, not a mandate to load six high-intensity physical sessions. Recovery, intensity and feasibility are individually managed. An under-resourced program may teach fewer days while documenting a **curriculum deviation** rather than claim it delivered NRY-013 as written.
