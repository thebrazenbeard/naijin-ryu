## Why this exists

The original Naijin-Ryū documents contain **far more real technical material** than the later 30-minute generic boxing-based starter cards. Their descriptive detail deserves respect. But repeated scientific absolutes, an idealized five-step response, unsupported effectiveness claims, inconsistent ways of measuring proficiency, and pressure to grade by time weaken the method. V2 keeps the identifiable Naijin-Ryū structure while demanding observable evidence and appropriate qualified teaching.

### Changes worth criticizing hardest

- **SURGE Loop:** The original integration example says a practitioner **may** combine S→U→R→G→E; it was never a mandatory ritual. The **optional V2** loop makes Environment and ethical reassessment more explicit. It has **not** been shown to outperform a simpler avoidance-first model.
- **Contact truthfulness:** striking, locks, throwing and grappling technical families remain source-defined; V2 will not certify or publish unsupported harmful detail as if text makes training safe.
- **Rule of Tens:** a ceiling on distinct *core* skills, not fifty presumed successful techniques or a statistical “three failures” rule.
- **Assessment:** 24-week planning and original Sashes remain; competence must be observed in specified conditions; rank/teaching authority is never automatically conferred.
- **Research:** neither concussions nor neck injuries are “nonlethal and harmless”; meditation effects, stances and self-defense outcomes are not scientifically guaranteed by confident prose.
