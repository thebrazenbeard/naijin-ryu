## External literature crosscheck, not proof of SURGE

The principal external references in V2 were independently looked up on the open web:
- [Amsterdam concussion consensus, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106898) — relevant to head-impact injury management, not a study of Shock;
- [Hyler et al., 2026 scoping review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jocn.2025.111811) — case-report-based evidence of potentially serious cervical vascular injury in martial contexts, **not** exposure-normalized incidence;
- [Lockhart et al., 2022 ukemi systematic review](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19074259) — adult judo kinematics, not Naijin-Ryū safety qualification;
- [Sánchez-García, 2025 teaching framework](https://doi.org/10.18002/rama.v20i1.2503) — theoretical combat-sport pedagogy, not a controlled superiority test;
- [Clark et al., 2019 constraints-led systematic review](https://doi.org/10.1177/1747954118812461) — interceptive sports literature with methodological limitations;
- [Senn et al., 2015 resistance-education trial](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa1411131) — evaluated a **different** program, not this martial art;
- [CDC 2025 adult activity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/adding-adults/) — general physical activity guidance, not prescribed SURGE duration.

These verified sources justify **caution and testing**, not naming Naijin-Ryū “validated.”
