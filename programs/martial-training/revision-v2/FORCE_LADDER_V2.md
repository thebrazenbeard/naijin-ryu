# Naijin-Ryū V2 — Force Ladder preservation and safety repair

**STATUS: PROPOSED EDITORIAL RECONCILIATION.** The source's **five-rung Force Ladder** is an original feature of Naijin-Ryū; it is **not** replaced by the V2 SURGE Loop. This document preserves its *named categories* while correcting unsafe or unsupported assumptions about outcomes. It is not a use-of-force legal standard, operational permission, or technique instruction.

**Primary source:** `NRY-007`, *Naijin-Ryū Manual*, §VII, **The Force Ladder**, paragraphs headed “The Force Ladder rungs in Naijin-Ryū are” through the ethical explanation immediately afterward. Read directly from the locally held original on **2026-10-22**. Original source wording and descriptive maneuver details remain in private custody.

## Two different models, two different questions

- **Force Ladder (§VII):** an *ethical classification* of different contemplated degrees of protective action, emphasizing necessity and stopping when the threat ends. The manuscript gives five ascending **named categories**; it does **not** itself certify that particular techniques are harmless, lawful or reliably effective.
- **SURGE (§III):** a *technical and perceptual vocabulary* for Shock, Unbalance, Reversal, Grapple and Environment. The manuscript includes an **illustrative integration flow** and says a practitioner **may** move through the pillars. It does not say every encounter must use all five.
- **V2 SURGE Loop:** an **EDITORIAL** real-time decision and feedback aid proposed for testing. It cannot override the authored Force Ladder or confer legal authority.

## The five original rungs, with revised evidence boundaries

| Original rung name | What the source is trying to distinguish | Revised caution / what must **not** be inferred |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Redirect (Avoid/Deflect)** | Avoidance, communication, moving out of a developing conflict, or minimal contact | Safety and escape are priorities when feasible. Even noncontact decisions require awareness of surroundings; verbal tactics are not guaranteed to work. |
| **2. Stun (Momentary Incapacitation)** | A source-described attempt to create a brief interruption of dangerous activity | **Do not claim a brief, benign, precisely timed stun.** Impacts can cause unpredictable severe harm. The source's one-second and non-injurious physiological assurances are **not verified**. No vulnerable-target instructions belong in a public general syllabus. |
| **3. Lock / Pin (Control)** | Attempts at restraint and positional control rather than continued striking | Holds, joint loading and pinning can injure or impede breathing, and may be unlawful outside specific circumstances. Do not treat “non-striking” as safe. Consent, qualified supervision and release rules apply in training. |
| **4. Mobility Denial (Tie-Up and Disable Movement)** | A source category for preventing continued movement or pursuit | Restriction of liberty and prolonged restraint create **substantial legal, medical and escalation risks**. This proposed course does **not** teach tying, immobilizing or disabling a person. The category's inclusion does not establish necessity, safety or authorization. |
| **5. Last Resort — Break (Selective Destruction)** | A high-consequence category reserved in the text for extraordinary threats | **Not a routine skill or rank privilege.** Severe injury cannot be called selective, harmless, automatically justified or reliably controllable. No injurious methods are reproduced. Any real-world decision is governed by actual law and circumstances, not a rank or source manual. |

**Editorial decision:** Keep the original five names in the source map and when discussing the ethic, but treat **Rungs 2–5 as increasingly high-consequence categories, not automatic steps to perform**. They are **not** a medical risk scale or a universal legal continuum. Circumstances, feasibility of withdrawal, threats to others, and applicable law cannot be compressed to a single chart.

## A safer training application

In low-risk classroom or paper-based scenarios, invite a learner to:
1. identify whether a safe route away or opportunity to seek help exists;
2. explain why more force **may not be needed**, including when the encounter already ended;
3. identify an *unknown* they would need competent external advice about;
4. articulate when practice must stop on withdrawal of consent.

A learner must not be required to demonstrate any of Rungs 2–5 through harmful contact for a classroom ethics check. This document does not supply legal advice on which rung a person “should use” against a stated threat.

## Core source-fidelity correction

The initial V2 draft emphasized an Environment-first decision loop **but omitted the source's specifically named Force Ladder**. That omission was a loss of authored structure. The same draft sometimes presented the original SURGE flow as **compulsory** when the primary text instead uses an illustrative “may.” Both are corrected here. The V2 Loop remains a testable *additional coaching aid*, not a replacement for §III or §VII.

> **Hostile reviewer:** Isn't keeping a five-rung ladder dangerous because it may be mistaken for a legal or medically reliable escalation guide?

**Partially accepted.** The five names are source history and should not be erased by an editor. But preserving them requires **prominent, repeated boundaries**: no stepwise mandate, no efficacy guarantees, no training authorization, and no legal justification supplied. A future operating curriculum should obtain qualified legal and martial safety review before adopting any live scenario interpretation.
