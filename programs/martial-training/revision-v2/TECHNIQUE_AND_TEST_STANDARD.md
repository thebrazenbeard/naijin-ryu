# Naijin-Ryū V2 — Technique Library, Rule of Tens and Adversarial Testing

**Status:** PROPOSED EDITORIAL STANDARD. The original manual's Rule of Tens remains a teaching principle; this document does not assert that fifty independently verified Naijin-Ryū techniques already exist. It also does not authorize live fights, unprotected throwing, neck restraints, head impact or dangerous surprise scenarios. Related: [Revised martial manual](NAIJIN_RYU_MARTIAL_MANUAL_V2.md).

## 1. What counts as a core technique

A **technique** is a reproducible physical or decision skill applied to a particular problem under defined conditions, *not* merely an inspiring label. A **drill** is a method for teaching or measuring it; one technique can have multiple drills. A **combination** is a contingent interaction between techniques, not automatically a new entry. A **pillar** is a category. A **sash** is a progression designation. Keeping them separate prevents artificial inflation.

Each pillar can contain **zero to ten** entries in its core set. Overlap is allowed, but the entry lives in one primary location and cross-references others. No technique should remain “core” solely because it sounds decisive or is associated with a traditional lineage.

## 2. Provisional source-aligned library

These are **families to audit**, not a claim of a completed ten-per-pillar register. The high-risk portions of the original technical manual remain subject to private professional review.

| Pillar | Source-backed family to inventory | Revised low-risk starting observable task | What is expressly **not** certified |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S** | Guard, burst-step entries, palm-heel / elbow technical families (NRY-007 §III/§XII; NRY-013 Phase 1 Shock) | Return to protective guard and recoverable stance after controlled short step or noncontact motion | Impact/stun physiology, power against a person |
| **U** | Stance/base, kuzushi, off-balance, sweep/throw concepts (NRY-013 Phase 1 Unbalance) | Recover own base after safe weight transfer; recognize partner movement without inducing a fall | Ability to sweep or throw |
| **R** | Angle/pivot, body redirection, wrist-control concepts (NRY-013 Phase 1 Reversal) | Choose a safe turn and route on a disclosed cue | Completion of a lock on resisting person |
| **G** | Clinch and ground positional concepts; ukemi prerequisites (NRY-013 Phase 1 Grapple) | Maintain personal space, stop/release, identify path back upright | Any choke, head/neck manipulation, ground fight readiness |
| **E** | Exits, surface, obstacle, bystander awareness (NRY-007 §III; NRY-013 Phase 1/2) | Identify two paths and reject a blocked route | Real threat awareness or safe defense of strangers |

### Candidate card S-01: Guard-to-recovery

- **Status:** EDITORIAL TEST CANDIDATE applying source guard and stance concepts, *not* an original named combination.
- **Conditions:** noncontact cleared floor, participant comfortable, ordinary shoes or bare feet appropriate to the surface.
- **Task:** from balanced neutral guard, take a short chosen step, return to steady posture with hands available to protect; stop immediately on agreed cue.
- **Measure:** proportions of trials showing balance, no foot crossing, immediate stop and deliberate return; record each separately.
- **Counterconditions:** different lead foot, cue after a brief pause, safely changed step direction.
- **Failure:** stumble, cannot stop, discomfort, confusion about exit, rushed arms.
- **Risk response:** remove speed, use support or stop; do not “push through.”
- **Not tested:** strike impact, real defense, partner resistance.

### Candidate card U-01: Base-and-recovery

- **Status:** EDITORIAL TEST CANDIDATE applying source kuzushi *principle*, not a claim of throwing skill.
- **Conditions:** stable flooring, controlled self-weight shifts, optional fixed support.
- **Task:** move center slightly off neutral then regain comfortable stable support before any next movement.
- **Measure:** repetition count completed with obvious recovery; note use of support and any discomfort.
- **Counterconditions:** forward/back versus lateral movement; test separately, no unexpected perturbation.
- **Failure:** hanging on another person, overbalancing, pain.
- **Not tested:** throws/trips or overcoming strength differences.

### Candidate card E-01: Route choice under benign change

- **Status:** EDITORIAL TEST CANDIDATE grounded in source Environment principle.
- **Conditions:** clear non-hazardous room or paper diagram with two visible exit routes; no ambush.
- **Task:** select a route, receive an announced “route now blocked” cue, pause and choose the viable alternative.
- **Measure:** correct selection, avoided obstacle, stop respected, actual verbal reasoning, failures and time if relevant.
- **Counterconditions:** different obstacle positions; comparable choice difficulty.
- **Not tested:** actual assault, weapon threat, crowd behavior, emergency evacuation certification.

These cards are **illustrations of evidence design**. They are **not** substitutes for qualifying the original striking, throwing, locking or grappling technique families.

## 3. Failure analysis instead of a magical number

The source tradition proposes a **Rule of Tens** and has discussed retiring techniques after repeated failed pressure tests. **Three failures are informative but not a scientifically sufficient universal statistical threshold.** A single severe risk failure can justify immediate suspension; dozens of successful cooperative repetitions may still prove almost nothing about resistance.

A failure review asks:
1. Was the intended task observable, or was a subjective effect presumed?
2. Was the movement physically impossible under these conditions, or simply not yet learned?
3. Did the student try a different technique because the first was a bad choice?
4. Did supervision, protective equipment or consent break down?
5. Did the failure happen under a rare edge case or a common condition?
6. Would a simpler established skill serve better with less injury risk?

Permissible decisions are **retain narrowly**, **coach differently and retest**, **demote to optional variant**, **restrict to qualified settings**, or **retire**. Log rationale rather than inventing binary pass/fail certainty.

## 4. Progressive evidence ladder

| Stage | Training design | Evidence it may support | Evidence it cannot support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Written hypothesis | Source family, risk and task specified | Clarity, source traceability | Physical performance |
| B — Solo prototype | Benign movement and decision exercise | Individual technical mechanics under observed conditions | Partner timing, contact response |
| C — Cooperative coach | Qualified/supervised partner work and safe boundaries | Ability to execute a taught movement with cooperation | Opponent resistance |
| D — Bounded variation | Only approved safe changes to starting position/partner cue | Adaptation inside the tested boundary | All real-world threats |
| E — Independent comparison | Same safe tasks, alternative teaching method, uninvolved observer | Comparative *task performance* under a reproducible design | Overall “best art,” live safety guarantee |

No stage uses unconsented participants or simulated criminal violence. Physical contact remains conditional on qualified review. **Report the number of people and tasks**; a percentage without denominator is not a performance finding.

## 5. Pilot study plan: test whether SURGE earns its complexity

**Core hypothesis:** learners taught the revised SURGE **functions + Environment-first decision loop** will show safer, more adaptive *noncontact* choices under benign changes than learners taught a simpler “notice/leave/help” rule.

**Competing explanation:** the simple three-word rule performs equally well or better. In that case, SURGE's additional taxonomy may be a source-faithful study framework but may not improve decision performance.

**Design concept (not authorized human research):** obtain appropriately qualified, consenting independent teachers; define comparable adult participants and experience; set two low-risk instructional conditions; randomize teaching order if reasonable; keep assessment scenario instructions/complexity equivalent; use blinded scorers where possible; track missing data, adverse events, and cue familiarity. Pre-specify outcomes such as route choice, stopping, recall and task-specific movement control. Analyze both absolute results and failures. **Do not call this a trial or start recruiting based on a public document**; obtain real ethical/governance review when warranted.

**Reject or revise** the claim if no improvement is observed, if errors worsen with the more complex terminology, or if valid comparison is impossible. A favorable result on a noncontact route game supports that game only, not self-defense efficacy.

## 7. Hostile conclusion

> **Reviewer:** The Rule of Tens sounds scientific because it is a number, but does the method actually benefit from such a ceiling?

**Unresolved; test it.** Ten is an authored simplicity constraint, not an optimum established by research. It becomes worthwhile if it reduces redundant teaching without leaving crucial tasks uncovered. Preserve the constraint as design intent, but allow evidence to show whether the set of *core entries actually needed* is smaller than ten or whether the classification needs revision.
