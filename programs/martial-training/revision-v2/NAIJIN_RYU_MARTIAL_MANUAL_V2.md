# Naijin-Ryū — Martial System, proposed technical revision V2
## Guard Life, Not Ego · SURGE · Sashes of Dawn

**Status: PROPOSED EDITORIAL REWRITE / NOT AN AUTHOR-ADOPTED EDITION.** 2026-10-22. Authored source texts remain unchanged, and the existing detailed daily schedules are **not replaced** by this proposal. This revision makes the art teachable, testable and ethically consistent without claiming invented combat results. It is not an instructor credential, validated self-defense program or medical clearance.

# Part I — Identity, purpose and ethical limits

## 1. What Naijin-Ryū is

Naijin-Ryū (Inner-God Style) is an authored martial discipline that studies **protective decision-making, distance, bodily organization, technical responses, controlled disengagement, and responsibility for consequences**. It is not a claim of guaranteed dominance. It draws on existing martial traditions and combines their concepts into its own training architecture. Its originality rests primarily in how it selects, organizes, teaches and judges behavior; it has not yet established independently measured superior effectiveness.

The art has **four ethical commitments**, **five SURGE pillars**, **five Sashes of Dawn**, and a multi-phase learning pathway. Their roles must stay distinct:

- **Ethics:** determine when action is justified and when it should stop.
- **SURGE:** classify physical and perceptual problems encountered under changing conditions.
- **Technical library:** describes teachable options that must earn their place through safe observation.
- **Sashes:** represent documented competence, judgment and responsibility, not invulnerability or sacred status.
- **Personal practice:** may include the original Morning Forge, Evening Stillness and reflective rituals; such participation is voluntary and never a substitute for skill or medical evaluation.

The **Six Aeons** of True Ascension are a different spiritual framework. No assertion about a person's Aeon or inner life establishes martial competence. Conversely, a martial student is not required to adopt religious or metaphysical beliefs.

## 2. The four commitments, in operational language

### Guard Life, Not Ego

Prefer avoiding unnecessary confrontation, creating distance, leaving safely, protecting bystanders and seeking help. If physical protection becomes unavoidable, decisions must account for immediate circumstances and consequences; stop when the justification stops. **A controlled drill is not a legal opinion, and no technique is automatically harmless or lawful.** Instructors assess observable restraint and choice, not self-reported moral purity.

### Centerline Ascension

**Martial reading:** organize posture, head, trunk, base and hand protection so that balance remains recoverable before and after movement. There is no single universally correct foot angle or torso alignment. A student should explain when and why they change posture for a task. **Spiritual reading:** the centerline remains an authored metaphor for self-organization, not a physiological measurement of enlightenment.

### Shadow Integration

Recognize stress, anger, fear, tunnel attention and overconfidence without rewarding escalation. Learn to pause, notice mistakes and correct them. Students are not required to disclose trauma, assign archetypes, accept psychological labels or undergo clinical treatment in class.

### Skill as Sacrament

Practice deliberately; protect the training partner; make corrections without humiliation; use skill in service. The original devotional framing remains available to consenting participants. It does not authorize coercion or make technical feedback a spiritual verdict.

## 3. A decision rule that outranks any technique

**Notice → choose the safest feasible response → check whether it worked → stop or change.**

1. **Notice:** environment, nearby people, pathways, physical contact, escalation, personal condition and uncertainty.
2. **Choose:** non-engagement or verbal boundary first when feasible; move away or get assistance; only use physical techniques appropriate to lawful and immediate protection if avoidance is unavailable.
3. **Check:** did distance increase, the threat cease, a path open, or a bystander become endangered?
4. **Stop / change:** do not continue because a prescribed sequence has more steps. Reassess after every meaningful change; seek emergency assistance when appropriate.

**Falsification question:** If the best outcome is achieved at the first step, is continuing through Unbalance, Reversal and Grapple justified? **No.** Importantly, the original manual **does not order an automatic five-technique chain**: NRY-007 §III says a practitioner **may** sequence pillars. The proposed V2 loop simply makes optionality, feedback, stopping and environment more explicit; it is an **editorial refinement**, not a correction of an actual five-step command.

### The original Force Ladder is retained separately

NRY-007 §VII already sets out **Redirect (Avoid/Deflect), Stun (Momentary Incapacitation), Lock / Pin (Control), Mobility Denial (Tie-Up and Disable Movement), Last Resort — Break (Selective Destruction)** as five named categories of escalating force. V2 **preserves that authored structure** in [Force Ladder V2](FORCE_LADDER_V2.md) while rejecting the original source's unsupported assurances that selected impacts are “non-injurious,” or that contact or restraint can be mechanically safe in all circumstances. The Ladder discusses ethical severity; SURGE organizes technical problems. **Neither gives legal permission or requires escalating after an available safe exit.**

# Part II — The revised SURGE engine

## 4. What SURGE means

The original names remain protected:

| Pillar | Revised functional question | Low-risk teaching task | What is not established |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S — Shock** | Can I interrupt a developing action or create the moment needed to protect and disengage? | Deliberate guard, timing cues, pad-oriented striking fundamentals under qualified supervision, rapid return to balance | Guaranteed stun, neurological shutdown, non-injurious impact |
| **U — Unbalance** | Where is *my* center of mass, and how do base and movement affect another person's balance in trained contexts? | Supported self-balancing, stance shifts, coached cooperative kuzushi with stop signal | That leverage always defeats size, unpredictably moving partners, or force differences |
| **R — Reversal** | Can a changed line, timing or direction improve safety instead of meeting pressure with pressure? | Angle steps, pivots, partner cue recognition, safe hand-position tasks | Reliable wrist-lock completion against arbitrary resistance |
| **G — Grapple** | At close range, can I preserve structure, avoid needless harm, create space, and leave when possible? | Positional literacy, contact boundaries, guided escape movement, appropriate mat-based fundamental grappling | That every pin/submission is safe, or ground commitment is best with multiple threats |
| **E — Environment** | What do surfaces, exits, obstacles, bystanders, visibility and additional people change about my choice? | Observe exits, identify hazards, choose routes, stop when movement would endanger others | That simulated scenario competence transfers automatically to real violence |

**Environment is always active.** It gains a dedicated session in Phase 2 but is not a final action performed after Grapple. Every other pillar is constrained by E.

## 5. Clarifying the illustrative integration flow with a conditional map

The source's **illustrative S → U → R → G → E integration flow** is worth teaching as one way to understand how tasks connect; NRY-007 §III says a practitioner **may** take such a path, rather than **must**. It is not a proven optimum against resistance. The **additional proposed SURGE Loop** is:

`ENVIRONMENT + ETHICAL CHECK → CURRENT PROBLEM (S / U / R / G / none) → ACTION → OBSERVE RESULT → EXIT / REASSESS`

Examples of legitimate outcomes:
- Recognize a blocked doorway and choose an open one: **E → exit**, no contact.
- Maintain a conversational safety boundary and step away: **E + R → disengage**.
- During a *supervised training exchange*, preserve balance despite a partner's movement: **U + R**, then stop on the agreed cue.
- During a *supervised ground session*, recover an upright position and space: **G → E → exit**, if the pathway is safe.

These are **instructional classification examples**, not guaranteed live self-defense scripts.

**A pillar is not a technique.** A strike could affect timing, balance or distance; an escape could require repositioning before contact. Overlap is expected. A technique earns a primary pillar only to help curriculum navigation; its effectiveness is judged by its actual task and observed result, not by its label.

## 6. A task-centered technique entry

Each candidate technique must have an individual card with:
1. **Name and provenance** (source location, influences, alternate names).
2. **Primary task:** exactly what problem it attempts to solve.
3. **Starting conditions:** distance, surface, controlled partner behavior, available equipment, permitted contact.
4. **Observable result:** e.g. recovered own balance or exited a designated lane; avoid unobservable “neural shock.”
5. **Failure modes:** range failure, loss of base, partner disengagement, blocked path, fatigue, pain, unexpected resistance.
6. **Injury and consent boundaries:** vulnerable areas, partner permissions, stop signals, contraindication review; safe supervision category.
7. **Coaching variants:** no-contact, cooperative, controlled representative practice; increasing complexity only with trained oversight.
8. **Evidence status:** authored proposal, coached observation, repeated independent test, or unknown.
9. **Decision:** keep provisional / revise / restrict / retire, with rationale and reviewer.

The [technique and test standard](TECHNIQUE_AND_TEST_STANDARD.md) supplies sample cards and the complete evidence procedure.

## 7. Rule of Tens, honestly applied

The **Rule of Tens** stays as the source's upper bound: *at most ten core entries under each pillar*, **not** an instruction to fill fifty slots. An entry is core only if (a) it covers an important distinct task, (b) the safe teaching pathway is specified, (c) its claims are proportionate to evidence, and (d) an assessor can observe success and failure without inventing threat outcomes.

A **candidate** is not a core technique. A **drill** is not necessarily a technique. Variations are tracked under one technique unless they demand materially different decisions or risk controls. Some essential skills—especially avoidance, communication and fall protection—can cross pillars and should not be double-counted merely to fill quotas.

The older idea of retiring a movement after **three failed pressure tests** is replaced with a **failure review**: three failures may be a valuable warning, but a small sample alone cannot settle general effectiveness. Investigate task definition, coaching, partner conditions, injury risk and alternatives; serious safety failure can stop training immediately regardless of sample size.

# Part III — Guard, stance and movement

## 8. Adaptive Guard Stance (Reactive Neutral)

**Source family:** NRY-016. The following is a **revised coaching specification**, not a claim that one posture performs best in every encounter.

- **Purpose:** protect the head and centerline without signaling avoidable aggression; keep walking or retreating available.
- **Feet:** comfortable stagger, approximately walking-step front to back, with enough lateral separation to prevent feet overlapping on ordinary steps. Either foot may lead; avoid forcing a universal heel-toe measurement or exact turnout.
- **Base:** knees softly flexed; weight can move in any direction without a large preliminary adjustment; heel lifting is task dependent, not a constant command.
- **Hands:** open or semi-open at roughly upper-chest to cheek height as context permits, elbows relaxed and not flared. A socially nonthreatening boundary hand may be forward; it is not a guaranteed protective shield.
- **Head/trunk:** eyes scan available space, jaw and shoulders relaxed; avoid exaggerated chin tucking or rigid, prolonged bracing.
- **Test:** step forward/back/laterally, pause on cue, return to a posture capable of moving again without crossing legs or excessive sway.

**Failure indicators:** stance so bladed that a step becomes awkward; hands dropped during transitions; excessive trunk lean; pain provoked by a compulsory angle. Correct by adjusting **one variable at a time**, then observe again.

## 9. Low Grappling Stance (Close-Quarters Flow)

**Source family:** NRY-016. The original text describes a wider, lower base and substantial foot turnout. This is **an adaptable range**, not the optimum prescription for every person's joints or every surface.

- **Purpose:** retain a recoverable base when contact is close and posture may be challenged.
- **Feet and depth:** use the width and knee/hip bend required by the task; do **not** prescribe an obligatory 45–90° turnout, 1.5–2 shoulder-width stance, or deep 90° squat. These remain historical source suggestions to be evaluated.
- **Hands:** open protective frame in front of the head/upper trunk, elbows inside a manageable line. Keep ability to release contact immediately on tap/verbal stop.
- **Spine/head:** keep a controllable trunk angle with view of partner and environment; never force neck or lower back positions merely to resemble a picture.
- **Movement:** small lateral repositioning, safe changes in level and return to upright. No prolonged low hold solely to satisfy a theoretical “more stable” appearance.
- **Test:** coached position-and-step tasks with no forceful pushing; later appropriately supervised, task-limited partner perturbations on suitable flooring.

**Failure indicators:** immobility, knee pain, loss of peripheral awareness, inability to separate from a partner, hands too distant to recover.

## 10. Transition principle: stability is recoverability

An apparently wide or low stance isn't automatically better. Compare two abilities: (a) hold position briefly **and** (b) change position deliberately. Teaching must include both, plus choosing to disengage. Stance figures are **illustrations of starting configurations**, not substitutes for observation of moving people.

# Part IV — Technical curriculum, without false precision

## 11. Shock: timing, structure and measured consequence

Begin with neutral guard and posture, then step/pivot timing and controlled hand motions. In an appropriately coached setting, progress to **non-head pad work** with deliberate contact, stable wrist/forearm and return to guard. The original syllabus includes palm-heel and elbow concepts; they are retained as source-defined technical families, not recast as generic jab/cross boxing.

**Not permitted as a scientific claim:** striking a named region will predictably “jolt the brainstem,” cause a harmless reflex stun, or reliably end aggression within a fixed number of seconds. Impact can injure. When a student cannot maintain controlled mechanics, the drill regresses; heavy striking and partner contact require separate competent coaching.

**Coaching outcome:** maintain own base and protect the training partner while meeting the selected target task under disclosed safe conditions. A drill measures *that*, not incapacitation.

## 12. Unbalance: first own base, then another's with consent

Start with static/dynamic self-balance, orientation and direction changes. Develop posture and cooperative partner sensitivity under close instructor oversight; transitions toward off-balancing or throws must use safe facilities, competent falling instruction and skilled partner control. **No unpadded throws, forced trips or partner surprises** in this general manual.

The principle “timing and leverage can matter more than force” is worth testing. “Size never matters” is not supported.

**Coaching outcome:** identify and recover base, take a safe line of movement, and stop before either person loses unsupported control.

## 13. Reversal: change the problem before insisting on the lock

Use safe footwork and angle selection to avoid opposition of force in a controlled setting. The source's joint-lock and redirection families remain part of the **historical technical catalog**, but entry, timing and safe release must be taught by appropriately qualified personnel with consent and resistance limits. No free-form application to an unwilling or untrained partner.

**Coaching outcome:** the learner perceives whether an option is viable and **abandons it when conditions change**. Demonstrating the full choreography against a cooperative partner is not evidence it will work against arbitrary resistance.

## 14. Grapple: positional control and the duty to disengage

Develop balance at close range, safe contact boundaries, orientation, positional transitions and escape choices. Once an appropriately trained instructor and suitable mats exist, ukemi and carefully constrained ground movement may be introduced. Keep conscious attention to standing up, access to exits, terrain and other people.

**No blanket “nonlethal” or “safe choke” guarantee.** Cervical compression and neck trauma present potentially serious risks; this public syllabus does **not** contain chokehold or neck-restraint instruction. Any further professional/sport-specific treatment requires qualified policy, medical risk consideration, informed agreement and lawful context.

**Coaching outcome:** stable positioning, immediate honoring of release/stop, and demonstrated capacity to disengage in the *specified exercise*. Do not infer that ground control is appropriate when multiple people or hard surfaces are present.

## 15. Environment: upstream information and continuous reassessment

Teach **routes, sightlines, surface choice, obstacles, crowd/bystander considerations, communication and help-seeking** beginning in Phase 1. In Phase 2, a dedicated E day tests decisions using harmless route-choice scenarios; in Phase 3, a qualified supervisor may add controlled variation that does not involve real weapons, ambushes or bystander risk.

**Coaching outcome:** explain the selected safe option, change it when information changes, and avoid a response that unnecessarily creates a new hazard.

# Part V — Training architecture

## 16. Three student phases plus Guardian, with authentic editions retained

The working **eight/eight/eight-week** phase arrangement remains the author's authorized editorial normalization of NRY-013. It is a **planning reference, not a rank clock**:

| Stage | Source rhythm | Teaching priority | Phase review: what an authorized assessor can observe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Phase 1 — Fundamentals Activation (weeks 1–8)** | Four day themes S/U/R/G, E embedded | Posture, safe distance, controlled footwork, basic source technical vocabulary, safety boundaries, recovery | Reliable basic movement; explains stop/exit; communicates consent and limits; not contact mastery |
| **Phase 2 — Pillar Integration (weeks 9–16)** | Five day themes S/U/R/G/E | Linking decisions across tasks, initial coached partner feedback where competent supervision exists | Adapts and separates techniques rather than robotically chaining; respects safety/partner limits |
| **Phase 3 — Dynamic Application (weeks 17–24)** | Five pillars plus separately supervised sixth session | Decision-making with benign variability, controlled appropriate resistance for qualified learners, debrief | Recovers from unfamiliar but safe variation; demonstrates restraint and de-escalation; no promise of live-threat competence |
| **Phase 4 — Guardian's Path (continuing)** | Individualized practice, mentorship | Technical upkeep, pedagogy, safeguarding, risk judgment, service and correction | Observed teaching capacity and independent oversight; no automatic instructor title |

**Source alternatives remain accessible:** NRY-012 six-week Fundamentals; NRY-007's slower Foundation lattice *and* its distinct faster implementation; NRY-010's individualized founder program. None is erased, and none is transformed into a generic mandate.

The full technical calendar and practical session structure appear in [Phase Curriculum](PHASE_CURRICULUM_V2.md). The original, longer daily syllabus is primary evidence for what the founder authored; later 25–30-minute noncontact session cards are **optional adaptations**, not the original system.

## 17. Coaching method: demonstrate, practice, vary, observe

For any permitted task, the cycle is:

1. State the task and what a successful **training outcome** looks like.
2. Demonstrate slowly under the relevant supervision and equipment conditions.
3. Practice safely and provide specific, observable feedback.
4. Vary **one** constraint (distance, pace, starting position or decision cue) **only after control is reliable**.
5. Repeat with changed constraints later rather than assuming a single performance shows learning.
6. Name what wasn't tested. Document adverse events and failed tasks as carefully as successes.

Constraints-led or representative learning designs are **research-informed options**, not a single scientifically proven pedagogy for Naijin-Ryū. Direct technique instruction, deliberate isolated rehearsal and coached variable practice can be compared fairly.

## 18. What a training session contains

A full martial session has **check-in and environment check → preparation → source-aligned technical work → appropriate variability → conditioning/recovery → debrief**. For a founder-intensive practice, the source's Morning Forge and Evening Stillness may bracket this training. A student choosing secular participation can omit all ritual and journaling without losing technical eligibility.

The main technical block should be recognizable as **Naijin-Ryū**, rather than replacing all source techniques with generic walking and jabs. When a drill is necessarily withheld or replaced for safety/equipment, **mark the substitution** and the resulting instructional gap. Protecting students is compatible with admitting the full art was not practiced.

# Part VI — Ranks and teaching authority

## 19. The five Sashes of Dawn remain

| Sash | Source title | Revised proposed **observable emphasis** (not automatic award) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Charcoal — Initiate** | Entry / humility | Explains four ethics, stops on cue, maintains basic personal space and controlled posture |
| **Brown — Foundation** | Roots / stability | Repeatable S/U/R/G foundational tasks, safe partners, aware of environment and self-limits |
| **Green — Growth** | Expansion | Can adapt source-aligned skills across appropriately controlled tasks; separates success from choreography |
| **Blue — Sky** | Broader view / responsibility | Integrates choices with environmental and ethical constraints, supports less experienced students under supervision |
| **White-Flare — Ascendant** | Compassion / service / mastery without ego | Demonstrated sustained technical control, teaching judgment, corrective humility and accountable service, through appropriate independent assessment |

These are **proposed assessment emphases** respecting the source names; they do **not** rewrite original rank meanings. Advancement requires **actual authorized assessors and documented observations**. Neither a GitHub file nor an AI can issue a Sash. The absence of a black belt remains part of the original design.

**Two non-negotiable failure conditions for any assessed task:** disregard of a real stop/withdrawal of consent; knowingly exposing a partner to an unmanaged safety risk. A learner's private theology, trauma history, medical records or willingness to journal are not assessment items.

## 20. Teaching is a separate qualification

A technically able student isn't automatically an instructor. Teaching eligibility needs observed explanation skill, corrections that work, safeguarding, emergency response procedures, incident records, conflict-of-interest management, capacity to refer outside expertise and real appointment by a legitimate authority. These are **requirements proposed for a future operating school**, not proof that such a school or authority currently exists.

When an assessment outcome is contested, review must be possible without making the original examiner sole judge. Any future youth program requires distinct trained adult supervision, safeguarding and regulatory assessment, not merely a martial sash.

# Part VII — Conditioning and mental practice

## 21. Physical preparation and recovery

Strength, aerobic capacity, mobility, deliberate rest and sport skill have overlapping but distinct outcomes. **No set number of SURGE minutes automatically equals national cardiovascular guidelines.** Plan progressive effort appropriate to the participant's baseline, manage fatigue and assess recovery; stop if concerning symptoms arise. Individual history and clearance remain private; historical founder-specific postoperative timelines must never be applied as universal medical prescriptions.

If the student cannot safely roll or perform a sprawl because equipment or qualified supervision is unavailable, do a clearly labeled **preparatory substitute**, not claim the missed skill is mastered. Falling skills must be learned under real controlled conditions; the existence of a diagram or mattress does not qualify them.

## 22. Morning Forge, mental discipline and Evening Stillness

The source includes breath, ukemi, Sun-Sprawl, contemplation and Triad journaling. **Retain those as original founder-centered practices**, optional for others. The separate NRY-009 mental program has **five scheduled days**: **Day 1 Shock, Day 2 Unbalance, Day 3 Reversal, Day 4 Grapple & Environment, Day 5 Environment**. The double Environment appearance is in the original source itself—not an editorial error to smooth away. This mental cycle is not the same rhythm as the Phase 1 four-day physical program.

Teach attention and self-report without claiming that nine breaths specifically “activate the vagus nerve,” that a journal “processes trauma,” or that imagery reliably rewires the amygdala. Such mechanistic or clinical claims require direct, appropriate evidence for the precise method. If reflection causes distress, opting out is valid.

### Revised mental questions (optional, not diagnostic)

- What did I actually observe versus assume?
- When did I choose not to escalate?
- Which movement failed and under what conditions?
- What would I do differently without endangering someone?
- What feedback do I need from a qualified person?

Store completed logs only in a controlled private location. A student can decline them without academic, rank or spiritual penalty.

# Part VIII — Proving what the art can and cannot do

## 23. Four different kinds of claim

1. **Authored value:** “Guard Life, Not Ego.” A design commitment, not a laboratory result.
2. **Technical mechanism hypothesis:** “this step can help change the usable angle.” Must be tied to conditions and task.
3. **Observed training outcome:** e.g. a learner regained balance in a precisely described cooperative drill.
4. **Real-world effectiveness claim:** e.g. Naijin-Ryū reliably improves safety under violence. **Not established by existing manuals or by test documents alone.**

A five-step sequence performed cleanly on a cooperative partner supports at most **a bounded training demonstration**, not predictable incapacitation or defense against violence. Techniques can be worth studying despite unverified field claims, provided their risks and uncertainties are stated.

## 24. Scientific corrections adopted in this proposal

- Delete certainty about **brainstem jolts**, guaranteed “neurological shutdown,” harmless head strikes and precise reaction-time superiority without task-relevant data.
- Replace “nonlethal = safe” with **“intended to minimize harm; risk cannot be eliminated”**. Head impacts and neck restraints can result in severe outcomes.
- Do not treat deeply turned-out feet or a fixed low stance as universally optimal biomechanics.
- Do not use unverifiable psychological mechanisms or rigid prescribed macronutrient percentages as evidence of martial method.
- Do not equate “our techniques survived three tests” with effectiveness; collect conditions, repeated trials, failures, independent observers and alternative explanations.
- Do not infer established authority from source PDF references, model-generated bibliographies, a Sash name, or a favorable testimonial.
- Do not imply that measured benefit for another self-defense education program proves benefit for Naijin-Ryū.

For cited evidence, boundaries and what it **cannot** support, see [Research and Claims Audit](RESEARCH_AND_CLAIMS.md).

## 25. Safe adversarial evaluation

Do not pressure-test by creating uncontrolled violence, hidden weapons, head impacts, choke exposure or unconsented throws. Instead, use supervised representative tasks with **known safeguards**, such as different routes, a partner changing a permitted movement cue, unexpected-but-benign stopping signals and constrained contact drills at instructor-qualified levels.

Compare against **simpler competing instruction**, not just a strawman. For example, does the full SURGE vocabulary produce better identification of escape choices than a minimal “notice/leave/help” curriculum? If not, the additional complexity may need revision.

An evaluation must record:
- definition of task and success/failure,
- training background and relevant selection criteria (without public identities),
- environment and supervision,
- permitted contact and actual exposure,
- number of attempts and variation,
- adverse events and stops,
- an independent observer's scoring when possible,
- results that contradict the hypothesis.

No exercises involving unprotected throws, choke/neck compression, head strikes or dangerous force are authorized by this proposed review manual.

## 26. Policy for the restricted last-resort material

The separate **Clear Intent** text exists as a historical high-consequence source. Its label, tactical assertions or connection to White-Flare do **not** create an automatic advanced training tier in a public course. No detailed dangerous techniques are reproduced here, no doctrine of inevitable harmlessness is attached, and ordinary instructor/rank progression never constitutes authorization for their use.

Any future handling of that material requires a separate expert, legal, safety, publication and access determination. This rewrite intentionally defines the boundary without publishing an operational appendix.

# Part IX — What has to happen before adoption

This is a strong **proposed rewrite**, not a finished or field-qualified martial art. The author may accept or reject each editorial choice. The primary source manuals and their provenance remain intact.

**Review order:** (1) confirm source wording/meaning against privately held originals once accessible; (2) obtain author acceptance of actual doctrinal changes; (3) review biomechanics, coaching and safety with qualified outside practitioners; (4) pilot only low-risk tasks and document failure; (5) validate specific claims before marketing, grading or operating.

### Strongest hostile objection

> **Reviewer:** By replacing confident combative language with observation and safety constraints, haven't you reduced Naijin-Ryū to etiquette and footwork—removing what makes a martial art martial?

**Partially accepted.** A curriculum limited to noncontact drills cannot validate genuine martial capability. The answer is **not** to relabel those drills as the whole art. This manual preserves the original technical families and conditions for supervised partner work; it explicitly marks the gap until appropriate coaches, equipment and observations exist. Ethical restraint cannot replace physical competence, and physical competence cannot excuse foreseeable injury.

> **Reviewer:** Is SURGE needed at all? Couldn't competent wrestling, boxing and judo coaching plus a simple avoidance rule outperform this custom layer?

**Unresolved.** That is a legitimate baseline comparison. SURGE earns a distinctive *instructional* role only if its categories improve specific decisions, retention, adaptability or safety without unnecessary complexity. Preserving its name is a source-fidelity choice, not proof of superiority.
