# Naijin-Ryū V2 — Scientific and safety claim repair ledger

**Status:** REVIEWED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE + EDITORIAL CORRECTIONS as of **2026-10-22**. Not a substitute for a professional risk assessment or independent study of Naijin-Ryū. This document **does not certify** the martial system or any individual practice. Original authored text is preserved in source custody. All proposed replacement wording is bounded by what available evidence can support.

## 1. How evidence may be used

**SOURCE CLAIM** = a statement recorded in an authored Naijin-Ryū document. **EXTERNAL EVIDENCE** = research or guidance concerning a specific analogous context. **INFERENCE** = a proposed application, not proven Naijin-Ryū effectiveness. **UNKNOWN** = not measured or source wording requires new review.

External publications about judo, BJJ, concussion, sport pedagogy or another self-defense program are **not** experiments on SURGE. Case reports describe possibilities and mechanisms, not population incidence. An authored religious claim or philosophical principle is not refuted merely by lacking a neurological marker, but clinical/physiological statements need verification independent of tradition.

## 3. Research register — what was checked

### E1. Amsterdam 2022 consensus, published 2023

Patricios et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine* 57(11):695–711. DOI **10.1136/bjsports-2023-106898**. Consensus synthesizes systematic review work on sport-related concussion; recommends removal from play and appropriate healthcare evaluation when concussion is suspected, and individualized decisions for contact sports. **Strong relevance to rejecting harm-free head-impact claims.** Not a trial of Naijin-Ryū technique or a rate-of-injury estimate for the system.

### E2. Vascular injuries of head and neck in martial arts, published 2026

*Journal of Clinical Neuroscience* 144:111811. DOI **10.1016/j.jocn.2025.111811**. Scoping review covers **31 papers, 40 reported cases**; mechanisms included chokeholds, direct blows and whiplash. Many reports were single cases. **Serious potential outcomes exist; frequency cannot be inferred** because exposure totals and reporting rates are unknown. This supports a caution and competent risk-policy requirement, not a claim that all grappling is too dangerous to teach.

### E3. Ukemi biomechanics systematic review, 2022

*A Systematic Review on the Biomechanics of Breakfall Technique (Ukemi) in Relation to Injury in Judo within the Adult Judoka Population*. DOI **10.3390/ijerph19074259**. Reviews 16 biomechanical studies totaling 158 judoka; reports different kinematics for experienced/novice performers and a fatigue-related observation. **Supports the need for meaningful supervised falling instruction**. Does not prove all specific breakfalls prevent all injuries or generalize to every novice/venue.

### E4. Combat-sport constraints-led pedagogy, 2025

Sánchez-García, *Initiation to combat sports from the perspective of ecological dynamics: the Constraints-Led Approach*. DOI **10.18002/rama.v20i1.2503**. Theoretical account proposing guided modified games and combat motor literacy. **An instructional competitor to pure memorized choreography.** It is not a randomized superiority trial of SURGE.

### E5. Constraints-led learning systematic review, 2019

Clark, McEwan & Christie, *The effectiveness of constraints-led training on skill development in interceptive sports: A systematic review*. DOI **10.1177/1747954118812461**. Authors expressly investigate an evidence base whose benefits are not straightforwardly established. **Against overselling any one coaching methodology**, including the one proposed by this rewrite.

### E6. General physical activity, CDC 2025

CDC, *Adding Physical Activity as an Adult*, December 4 2025; and *What Counts as Physical Activity for Adults*. Describes adult guideline of 150 minutes of moderate activity and two strengthening days per week, and adjustment to individual health/capacity. **Not equivalent** to completed SURGE training minutes or a prescription for an individual with specific health history.

### E7. Meditation, NIH NCCIH

*﻿Meditation and Mindfulness: Effectiveness and Safety*, NCCIH. Reports possible benefits in some contexts but methodological limitations; adverse experiences such as anxiety can occur. **Cannot establish that Morning Forge's nine breaths or Triad journaling constitute medical treatment** or that any specific vagal/brain claim is true.

### E8. Self-defense education RCT, 2015

Senn et al., *Efficacy of a Sexual Assault Resistance Program for University Women*, *NEJM* 372:2326–2335. DOI **10.1056/NEJMsa1411131**. RCT of 893 first-year university women; one-year completed-rape reports 5.2% intervention vs 9.8% brochure control. The intervention integrated risk appraisal, verbal skills and physical education; this is important **proof that precisely specified education can be evaluated**. **Not proof that Naijin-Ryū or its techniques confer similar benefit.**

## 4. Scientific claim replacement language

Instead of:
- “A strike jolts the brainstem and reliably shuts down resistance.”

Use:
- “This striking family is studied for its placement, timing, biomechanics and controlled training application. Effects of impact on a person vary and may cause severe injury. Naijin-Ryū-specific incapacitation claims have not been verified.”

Instead of:
- “The grappling finish controls without harm.”

Use:
- “The objective is to protect people and minimize harm. Grappling, pressure, locks, falls and restraints carry risks that must be reviewed for the exact supervised task; safe disengagement is prioritized when feasible.”

Instead of:
- “Five breaths reset cortisol / regulate the vagus nerve.”

Use:
- “A brief breathing practice can serve as an optional attentional routine. Claimed physiological or therapeutic effects of this exact routine are unverified.”

Instead of:
- “A technique passed three stress tests.”

Use:
- “In three trials under [specified cooperative/restricted conditions], [observed outcome] occurred [N] times. The sample and conditions are insufficient to infer performance outside the observed task.”

Instead of:
- “Finishing Phase 3 demonstrates real-world readiness.”

Use:
- “Completion documents participation; task-specific evaluations document selected observed competencies. Real-world protective outcomes are unestablished.”

## 5. What evidence would be necessary to make stronger claims?

For each claim, require a question and method appropriate to its subject. For a movement task, define movement, comparison, qualified supervision and outcome. For injury prevention, collect appropriate denominators, person-time/exposure, injuries and independent review. For an education program, specify target population, comparable alternatives, ethical safeguards and outcomes prospectively. For a neurological or clinical mechanism, use relevant medical research; do **not** infer biology from martial imagery or a paragraph with numbered references.

Disconfirming results, adverse events, disagreements between assessors and participant withdrawals are part of the evidence. Keep personal and protected data out of public GitHub.

## 6. Residual unknowns and hostile self-review

- **UNKNOWN:** validity of specific biomechanics in NRY-016 for individual body types and surfaces.
- **UNKNOWN:** optimal teaching method or number of “core” techniques.
- **UNKNOWN:** professional qualification and safety controls for any future operated school.
- **UNKNOWN:** whether original PDF bibliographies correctly support the manuscript's strongest claims without a line-by-line source audit.

> **Reviewer:** Are you importing medical rules into a spiritual martial system and pretending all contact is indefensible?

**Rejected in part.** The ethical claims and symbolism remain authored. Where a text makes a **physical or medical assertion**, however, it is no longer exempt from physical evidence. The rewrite does not prohibit all contact; it refuses to *certify* any particular risky practice without qualified conditions and specific evidence.

> **Reviewer:** Is citing other martial arts literature enough to justify the new SURGE Loop?

**No; accepted.** The SURGE Loop is an **additional editorial decision aid**, not a replacement for an original mandatory tactical sequence: NRY-007 §III says a practitioner **may** combine the pillars in its illustrative flow. The aid and its complexity must be compared with simpler methods before superiority can be claimed.
