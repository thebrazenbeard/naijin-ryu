# Phase 1 — Session A: 30-minute solo restart

**Pillar:** Shock, understood here as **readiness, space and controlled movement**, not incapacitation. **Original structure:** `NRY-013` Phase 1 gives Shock the first of four weekly training days. `NRY-009` Day 1 gives the mental theme of observing an emotional surge. **Everything below — minute-by-minute blocks, cardiovascular intervals and punch limits — is an editorial re-entry design.** It is not the original contact technique syllabus or a certification program.

## Before starting (no equipment or partner needed)

Choose an obstacle-free floor and a route where walking or marching is safe. Check footwear, surroundings, fatigue and current comfort. If your body or relevant medical guidance does not support a movement, substitute walking or stop. This session deliberately requires **no bag, impact target, opponent, ground fall or partner**.

| Minutes | Segment | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0–5** | Prepare | Easy march or walk; comfortable shoulder and hip motions; ankle mobility; slow increases in temperature and breathing |
| **5–11** | Balance and footwork | Step forward/back and laterally at low speed; return to comfortable stance between steps; notice shoulders, breathing, center of gravity and footing |
| **11–19** | Four controlled shadowboxing rounds | Four **90-second** low-intensity rounds, with roughly **30 seconds** easy movement between rounds and time to reset. Work on guard, light straight-hand motions **only if comfortable**, distance, relaxed recoil and stepping away. No power test or injurious targeting |
| **19–26** | Cardio intervals | Alternate **one minute brisk / one minute easy** three times, then one more comfortable brisk minute. Walking or marching suffices; the faster minutes should still allow recovery and safe conversation |
| **26–30** | Reset and reflect | Walk easily; settle breath without forced breath-holding; note overall effort, balance, symptoms, and one useful adjustment for the next session |

The segment durations are approximate. Round transitions and rest can be lengthened while preserving a session of reasonable duration. **Do not rush to satisfy the clock.**

## Adaptations and stop criteria

For any returning student, keep technical movements at controlled, submaximal speed without contact. A punch motion is **optional**, not necessary to obtain the goal of posture and spacing. If one hand or shoulder is sensitive, use footwork/guard awareness instead of striking. Do not practice complex coupled punch-and-duck patterns unless they have been individually tolerated and safely reintroduced.

Stop the affected exercise for sharp pain, increased discomfort, sudden weakness, a feeling of joint instability, loss of balance, dizziness or unusual breathlessness. If symptoms persist or are concerning, seek medical guidance. Training status or an earlier clearance does not override new symptoms.

## What counts as success

Completion means **a controllable practice opportunity** with a useful baseline. Record what was attempted, what was modified, the session effort and next-day recovery. It does **not** certify self-defense competence, a sash, physiological mechanism, or readiness for higher-contact practice.

**Mental conditioning:** The source's Shock theme encourages noticing how one responds when attention is challenged. As an optional, low-stakes reflection, ask: *When did I start rushing, and what happened when I deliberately slowed down?* This is **editorial wording**, not a therapeutic protocol.
