# Phase 1 — Session C: Reversal and direction changes

**Source:** `NRY-013`, Phase 1 “Day 3: Reversal Training – Redirecting Force Fundamentals.” The source covers partner force redirection and joint controls. This public solo plan **replaces those combative techniques with benign movement-direction decisions**; it does not claim that the original supplied this particular sequence. `NRY-009` Day 3 supplies an optional mental-reflection theme.

**Target:** approximately **26 minutes**, solo, noncontact and without sudden attacks, partner grabs, joint manipulation or imposed resistance.

| Time | Segment | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0–5 min** | Prepare | Easy walk, gentle hip/ankle range and comfortable torso turns. |
| **5–13 min** | Slow directional pivots | Start in an easy stance; step and turn a modest amount, return to center, then try the other direction. Keep head, knees and feet moving comfortably. Pause before any loss of balance. |
| **13–19 min** | Choose a safer direction | Place a harmless visual marker on the floor or imagine a line. Without jumping or speed, practice noticing a preferred exit and stepping away from the line while retaining space to stop. |
| **19–23 min** | Easy conditioning | Walk at a conversational pace; slow down when needed. There is no required speed, heart-rate or distance target. |
| **23–26 min** | Reflection | Record which turn felt controlled, whether you needed to pause, and what you would change next time. |

## Technical and ethical interpretation

The relevant **SOURCE** principle is Reversal as responding by redirection rather than insisting on overpowering force. The drills above offer a **safe conceptual analog** only. They do not train functional defenses against punches, grabs or restraint and should not be marketed as such. Environmental awareness remains visible through paths, bystanders and opportunities to leave.

**Safety alternative:** if turning or shifting weight feels unstable, perform tiny step-and-return movements or substitute a straightforward walk. Avoid rapid spins, jerks and unpredictable floor obstacles. Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, instability or concerning symptoms.

**Optional mental theme:** the mental-conditioning source associates Reversal with questioning an initial reaction. The editorial prompt is: *When was a slower or simpler choice more useful than my first impulse?* Introspection is always optional.
