# Mental conditioning rotation for a four-session Phase 1 week

**Source:** `NRY-009`, *Naijin-Ryū Phase 1 Mental Conditioning Manual (Enriched Edition)*, which contains **five named themed days**: Shock; Unbalance; Reversal; Grapple & Environment; and Environment. The student movement manual `NRY-013` instead schedules Phase 1 physical work **four days a week**, assigning the four direct pillar sessions to Shock, Unbalance, Reversal and Grapple. **This is a genuine source-schedule distinction.**

**EDITORIAL harmonization:** keep *four* physical practice opportunities and distribute the **fifth mental Environment** topic across those four as an observation theme. Do not silently rewrite the original five-day mental syllabus into a source-authored four-day text.

| Physical session | Mental source topic | Optional 1–3-minute reflection *added editorially* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [A — Shock](phase-1-session-a.md) | Day 1: readiness in emotional surprise | What did I notice when movement became hurried, and could I choose to slow down? |
| [B — Unbalance](phase-1-session-b.md) | Day 2: centering and resilience | What helped me regain balance, physically or metaphorically? |
| [C — Reversal](phase-1-session-c.md) | Day 3: redirecting thought or response | Was my first interpretation the only reasonable interpretation? |
| [D — Grapple](phase-1-session-d.md) | Day 4: Grapple **and Environment** | Did I notice surface, space and exit choices before acting? |
| **Distributed across A–D** | Day 5: Environment (strategic awareness / mental terrain) | What changed about my attention when the surroundings changed? |

The source also includes breath attention, visualization, journaling, mantras and some categorical physiological explanations. For this public starter, **every mental practice is optional**, with no required trauma recollection, private confession, visualization or spiritual belief. A participant can complete movement work without doing any mental module.

## Source claims not promoted to evidence

The original discusses cortisol, amygdala, vagal regulation, heart-rate variability and claimed trauma processing effects. Those are **source assertions** and do not constitute proof of specific outcomes from these exercises. This track cannot diagnose mental-health conditions, perform trauma treatment, or determine a person's Aeonic stage.

A participant may choose brief present-moment breath awareness or skip it. Avoid imposed breath-holds or effortful breathing and stop introspective practice if it creates distress. Any actual clinical needs belong with qualified professionals.

## Evidence categories

**SOURCE:** five named mental day headings and their intended themes.

**EDITORIAL:** five-to-four redistribution, the 1–3 minute prompts, voluntary implementation and omission rules.

**EXTERNAL:** general mindfulness evidence, when separately referenced in [the existing mental-conditioning overview](../../docs/practice/mental-conditioning.md).

**UNKNOWN:** whether any participant benefited; whether exercise changed specific neurophysiological measures; whether a facilitator or clinical supervision exists.
