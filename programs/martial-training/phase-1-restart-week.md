# Phase 1 restart week — four adaptable solo practice opportunities

**Basis:** `NRY-013`, *Phases of SURGE Training Manual.docx*, Phase 1 (weeks 1–8, four days/week); `NRY-009`, *Phase 1 Mental Conditioning*, days 1–5; and the repository's [eight-week Foundation](../../docs/training/8-week-foundation.md). **EDITORIAL:** The short sessions, intervals, optional cardio and ordering below are newly constructed plans, **not quotations or mandatory prescriptions from the sources**.

This is a **re-entry week**, appropriate for assessing current conditioning after a gap. It is not equivalent to completing the original eight-week phase or passing a martial rank examination. Every session is individual, noncontact and subject to current readiness. The four sessions can be spread over more than seven days; recovery and modified activity count as legitimate choices.

## Week 1 plan

| Practice | Time window | Source-linked theme | Editorial solo practice | Outcome to record privately |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A — Shock / readiness** | 25–30 min | Defensive posture, spacing, attention | Mobility, stance/footwork, gentle noncontact shadowboxing, comfortable cardio intervals | Can maintain guard and controlled return to stance without worsening discomfort |
| **B — Unbalance / stability** | 20–30 min | Center of gravity, off-balancing concept | Gentle walking, stance transitions and low-risk balance tasks without pushing or tripping another person | Balance and recovery observed under safe conditions |
| **C — Reversal / redirection** | 20–30 min | Calm changes in movement direction | Noncontact pivots, moving away from a marked line, optional easy cardio | Can reverse direction without rushing or losing control |
| **D — Grapple / ground literacy** | 20–30 min | Safe spatial and ground awareness | Surface check, low-impact position changes and exits only as individually appropriate; no submissions or unsupervised falling/throws | Can identify safe surface, stop signal and when instructor help is needed |

**Environment** is an awareness theme across all four days: exits, people, obstacles, lighting, footing and the opportunity to avoid a conflict. It does not require simulating a real assault.

## Ready-to-run first-week sessions

- [A — Shock, 30 min](phase-1-session-a.md): gentle shadowboxing, footwork and cardio.
- [B — Unbalance, 25 min](phase-1-session-b.md): stable stance, supported balance and walking.
- [C — Reversal, 26 min](phase-1-session-c.md): controlled pivots, choosing exits and easy conditioning.
- [D — Grapple, 24 min](phase-1-session-d.md): safe positioning, distance and noncontact disengagement.

The public [training registry](../../data/training-restart-week-v1.json) defines the **105 planned minutes across all four sessions**, and [mechanical validator](../../tools/verify_training_restart.py) checks that segment times align with their respective documents. This is a proposed menu, not a requirement to finish 105 minutes in a week. The [progression gates](phase-1-progression-gates.md) decide whether to repeat, simplify or adjust training.

## Adjusting instead of escalating blindly

- Begin at an effort where conversation remains possible and technique remains controlled; numerical effort is an **editorial guide**, not a medical rule.
- If a movement aggravates pain, produces new weakness, instability, dizziness or other concerning symptoms, stop that activity. Sharp or persistent symptoms merit professional assessment rather than forced completion.
- A previously injured participant may need exercise modification and personalized clearance even if their overall exercise participation has been cleared. **Do not publish health histories in GitHub.**
- Never use contact sparring, head/neck strikes, submissions, actual takedowns, surprise threats or fatigue punishment as part of this standalone restart.
- If a day does not go as intended, repeat or scale it down rather than attempting to “make up” an unsafe workload.

## Recovery and continuation

Leave easy/rest days between demanding sessions when useful. At the end of the first week, review **consistency, technique control, conditioning and next-day recovery**, not claimed fighting capability. The source's four-day Phase 1 schedule is an authored design; no schedule demonstrates individual training readiness by itself.
