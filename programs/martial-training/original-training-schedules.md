# Original Naijin-Ryū training schedules — source-first reference

**Read this before selecting a derivative program.** Naijin-Ryū did **not** begin as a handful of generic boxing or mobility sessions. Its original documents already contain **daily schedules, exercises, technique families, conditioning, mental practices, recovery days and progression**. The original source files are held in private custody, not reproduced verbatim here. This reference describes their structure without republishing source-specific injury history or detailed injurious instructions.

This page is a **SOURCE MAP**. The individual physical and theological claims within a source are authored claims; they have not automatically been proven effective or medically appropriate. The author's permission to normalize phase lengths did **not** authorize replacing the contents of his training method with generic fitness work.

## Why the short solo cards look different

The [30-minute Phase 1 Session A](phase-1-session-a.md) and the [32-session eight-week solo course](phase-1-eight-week-course.md) were **newly authored EDITORIAL, lower-contact adaptations**. They are **not** the original Shock schedule or the complete Naijin-Ryū technical method. In particular:

- **Jabs and rear-hand crosses** in the short Session A are *optional generic shadowboxing substitutions*, **not a requirement or signature combination from the original Shock lesson**.
- **The original Shock source** emphasizes a distinct striking vocabulary, including palm-heel and elbow concepts, burst-step footwork, timing, controlled mechanics and transitions. A brief shadowboxing substitute omits much of that material.
- The derivative's 25–30-minute format may be useful for a re-entry workout, but **must not be presented as equal to** an original ~60- or 60–90-minute lesson.
- Week-by-week lesson cards built later are **editorial teaching designs**. They retain source pillars and the accepted eight-week timing but do **not** reproduce the source's partner, contact, falls, locks, grappling, tactical, or assessment progression.

## Selecting a training route honestly

**Original founder-oriented solo training:** start from **NRY-010** in authorized private custody, check which original requirements remain current for the individual, and distinguish optional rituals from physical training. Historic individualized limitations do not establish present readiness.

**Original six-week fundamentals:** follow the documented **NRY-012** source structure; do not assume a separate eight-week editorial course replaces its daily topics or physical instruction.

**Working standard student development:** use the original **NRY-013** Phase 1–3 source schedule for the art's pedagogical and technical identity. The normalized eight-week phase decision remains valid; actual contact technique training, assessment and supervision must be planned safely rather than inferred from the calendar.

**Short solo mobility/boxing re-entry:** use the derivative [Phase 1 restart](phase-1-restart-week.md) **only when deliberately choosing an alternative**, not by default when someone asks for “Naijin-Ryū training.”

## What should be improved next

1. Build a **source-by-source daily lesson index** from NRY-010, NRY-012 and NRY-013 before inventing another class sequence, keeping each version independent.
2. Inventory what source-specific movement, conditioning, safety, philosophical teaching and assessments each original day contains, with exact sections/pages where possible.
3. Add a **clearly separate current personalized training overlay** based on actual training feedback and current qualified advice; keep any personal health information private.
4. Only then decide where supplementary 30-minute plans, walking, accessibility adaptations or other safe substitutes fill a real gap.
5. Qualify contact instruction, falls and partner practice separately; neither documentation nor an exercise substitution proves martial effectiveness or grants a sash.

**Non-negotiable:** the originals remain source authority for what was authored; editorial plans remain labeled, revisable additions. Training-source provenance, operational safety, physical instructor qualification and rank authority are separate evidence classes.
