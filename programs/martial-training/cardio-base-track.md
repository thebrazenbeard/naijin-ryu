# Cardio base track — walking/marching companion for martial practice

**Purpose:** establish a sustainable low-impact aerobic habit that can accompany, but is not identical to, Naijin-Ryū's martial syllabus. **Evidence status: EDITORIAL + EXTERNAL**, not a training prescription reproduced from `NRY-013`. The original martial documents include conditioning themes but do not provide this graded walking protocol.

## Three interchangeable practice options

| Option | Total time | Suggested structure | When to choose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Short** | **10 min** | 2 min easy start; 5 min comfortable walking/marching; 3 min easy finish | When returning from inactivity, short on time or managing recovery |
| **Standard** | **15 min** | 3 min easy start; 9 min comfortable walking (optionally mixing short brisk periods); 3 min easy finish | When a short option is consistently comfortable |
| **Longer** | **20 min** | 4 min easy start; 12 min comfortable walk, optionally divided into shorter blocks; 4 min easy finish | When the standard option is reliably comfortable and recovery is satisfactory |

The times are **editorial examples, not minimum requirements**. Slow walking, indoor marching, a safe accessible route, or another individually appropriate low-impact aerobic activity can substitute. The options are **not exhaustive**: even a few minutes may be useful when that is what is feasible.

## Monitor exertion without pretending to measure fitness

The [CDC talk test](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html) describes relatively moderate activity as allowing conversation but not singing. A person who cannot comfortably speak should generally lower pace here. The [CDC start-slow guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/physical-activity/getting-started.html) favors starting with manageable activities and increasing gradually.

Both references are **external general physical-activity guidance**. They cannot establish that a particular participant is medically cleared, that their heart or joint response will be normal, or that this specific Naijin-Ryū session is clinically tested.

## How to combine it with the four SURGE sessions

- **A (Shock)** already contains approximately seven minutes of alternating comfortable/brisk walking or marching. Count actual aerobic minutes, not the full 30-minute martial session, if tracking aerobic activity volume.
- **B (Unbalance)** includes around five minutes of walking recovery.
- **C (Reversal)** includes around four minutes of easy walking.
- **D (Grapple)** includes around five minutes of easy conditioning.

The four martial workouts therefore contain **about 21 minutes of explicitly designated conditioning segments in total**, which may be partly easy rather than moderate intensity. Do **not** misrepresent all 105 minutes of the sessions as moderate aerobic minutes, or assume the 21 minutes meets federal weekly activity targets.

A separate 10-, 15- or 20-minute cardio option may be scheduled when it does **not** crowd out necessary recovery. No fifth martial SURGE day is created by doing separate easy cardio. It may also replace a martial session on a day when movement quality is poor or a modified day is appropriate.

## Progression rules

**Observe first.** If breathlessness, instability, fatigue or soreness is worse than expected, repeat a shorter option or recover. If several prior efforts are comfortable and next-day recovery is acceptable, consider modest increases in **duration before speed**, rather than simultaneously increasing frequency, intensity and complexity. If there are new or concerning symptoms, stop and obtain appropriate professional guidance.

For context only, [CDC adult activity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/adding-adults/) recommends a longer-term goal of 150 weekly minutes of moderate activity plus muscle-strengthening work on two days for most adults. **This is a population target, not an immediate assignment or a claim that these martial sessions fulfill it.**

## Documentation and privacy

Use the [blank private log](restart-session-log.md) to record what was *actually* walked or marched, conversation comfort and next-day recovery. Do not commit individualized health or training logs to the public repo.
