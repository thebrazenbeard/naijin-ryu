# SURGE — five martial training pillars

**Sources:** `Naijin-Ryū Manual .docx`, section III; `Phases of SURGE Training Manual.docx`.

SURGE names five linked areas of the Naijin-Ryū martial system:

| Letter | Pillar | Core subject |
|---|---|---|
| S | Shock | Immediate disruption and defensive readiness |
| U | Unbalance | Base, leverage, movement and kuzushi |
| R | Reversal | Redirection and adapting to incoming force |
| G | Grapple | Close-range control and positional awareness |
| E | Environment | Terrain, exits, bystanders and situational awareness |

The manual describes integrating the pillars in sequences, but not every situation requires all five. The **Rule of Tens** proposes no more than ten core techniques per pillar, favoring deep practice over a large unfiltered catalog.

## Proposed critical rewrite (not adopted doctrine)

A newly prepared [SURGE V2 martial rewrite](../programs/martial-training/revision-v2/README.md) retains **Shock, Unbalance, Reversal, Grapple and Environment**, but proposes that **Environment and the ethical decision continuously constrain pillar choice**, rather than treating S→U→R→G→E as a mandatory real-world chain. The **original** NRY-007 §III already uses an illustrative flow (“may”), not a five-step obligation. V2 clarifies decision-making rather than correcting a nonexistent mandate; it also restores the [original five-rung Force Ladder](../programs/martial-training/revision-v2/FORCE_LADDER_V2.md), treats neurological incapacitation assertions as unverified, makes stance geometry task-specific and calls for supervised falsifiable technique evidence. This is a **proposed editorial revision** under separate review, not a silent change to the original source chapter.

## Stance framework

`Stances for Naijin-Ryū.pdf` proposes two primary families: **Adaptive Guard Stance (Reactive Neutral)** and **Low Grappling Stance (Close-Quarters Flow)**. Both are presented as mobile foundations, with transitions and adaptation to context instead of a single fixed pose. The illustrations remain in the original source PDF.

## Method and evidentiary status

The author draws conceptual influences from a variety of martial arts. A claimed influence is **not** a verification of efficacy, comparative superiority or lineage endorsement. The manuscript includes physical explanations that have not been independently tested here.

This repository page is a taxonomy, not a live-combat instruction manual. See [curriculum](curriculum.md) and [ethos](ethos-and-progression.md).
