# External evidence review — 2026-10-21

This file separates **source assertions** from **independently checked external information**. It is not a comprehensive legal, medical or coaching qualification, and dates matter.

## R1 — Youth camp regulation in Indiana

**SOURCE ASSERTION:** `Camps Overview.docx` describes Indiana as a low-barrier state and states broadly that youth camps do not require state licensure.

**EXTERNAL EVIDENCE:** The Indiana Department of Health operates a Youth Camps program that **inspects qualifying youth camps and approves construction/alteration plans**. Indiana's 410 IAC 6-7.2 covers qualifying outdoor group living for **more than 72 continuous hours**, for **ten or more unaccompanied minors**, among other definitional criteria. The department provides a permit application for construction or alteration. Which permits and obligations apply to a particular event depends on its facts. The current public rule page specifies submission of scaled construction/addition/significant-change plans at least 90 days before initiation; this is not a complete permit determination.

- https://www.in.gov/health/eph/youth-camps/
- https://www.in.gov/health/eph/laws-rules-and-forms/410-iac-6-72-youth-camps/

**RESULT:** The blanket no-state-regulation implication is not defensible. No camp may be treated as launch-ready based on this draft. Relevant zoning, facility, fire, sanitation, supervision and safeguarding requirements require jurisdiction-specific review.

## R2 — Neck restraints and “harmless incapacitation”

**SOURCE ASSERTION:** Sections III and VII of `Naijin-Ryū Manual .docx` characterize some choke/carotid-restraint methods as quick or nonlethal and potentially without lasting harm.

**EXTERNAL EVIDENCE:** A 2026 scoping review of head-and-neck vascular injuries in martial arts documents serious cerebrovascular injury cases, including cases associated with chokeholds. A 2025 clinical review emphasizes uncertainty around long-term outcomes of vascular neck restraint, even while describing some existing sport safety data.

- Hyler et al., *J Clin Neurosci* 2026, PMID 41406831: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41406831/
- *The vascular neck restraint of mixed martial arts*, PMID 40202022: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40202022/

**RESULT:** **Do not label neck restraints harmless or safe incapacitation methods.** No detailed instructional sequence for causing unconsciousness is endorsed here, and supervised sport participation is not equivalent to real-world safe use.

## R3 — Mindfulness and breathing evidence

**SOURCE ASSERTION:** The Phase 1 mental-conditioning manual attributes specific and sometimes categorical effects on the vagus nerve, amygdala, cortisol, trauma processing and cognition to its practices.

**EXTERNAL EVIDENCE:** NIH NCCIH describes evidence for potential stress and mental-health benefits of some mindfulness and breathing practices, while noting mixed quality, uncertain mechanisms, variation among interventions and some adverse experiences. These findings do not validate the specific Naijin-Ryū exercises or guarantee an effect for a particular practitioner.

- https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety
- https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/stress

**RESULT:** Present breathwork and journaling as **optional supportive practices**. Avoid causal neurobiology claims without study-specific support, and never substitute them for clinical treatment.

## R4 — Sport head injury and return to participation

**SOURCE ASSERTION:** Some training material advocates increasingly demanding physical practice and sparring as the phase calendar progresses.

**EXTERNAL EVIDENCE:** CDC HEADS UP recommends immediate removal from sport for suspected concussion and provider-led gradual return, with stepwise progression.

- https://www.cdc.gov/heads-up/guidelines/returning-to-sports.html
- https://www.cdc.gov/heads-up/response/index.html

**RESULT:** Injury risk and symptoms outrank preset curriculum dates. Training progression must account for individual health, supervision and qualified medical review.

## R5 — Proposed Church formation and tax claims

**SOURCE ASSERTION:** The Church package contains prospective Indiana nonprofit corporation provisions, board rules, and plans concerning tax exemption.

**EXTERNAL EVIDENCE:** IRS materials distinguish legal-entity formation, exempt-purpose organizational documents, and federal tax rules. Churches meeting section 501(c)(3) requirements may qualify without seeking an IRS determination letter; many seek recognition voluntarily. Merely drafting corporate articles or describing the organization as a church does not establish that the relevant legal conditions have been met.

- https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/organizational-test-internal-revenue-code-section-501c3
- https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/churches-integrated-auxiliaries-and-conventions-or-associations-of-churches

**RESULT:** Do not represent draft organizational papers as proof of Indiana incorporation or IRS recognition. The board-composition statements in the source remain **unverified**, not editorially assumed.
