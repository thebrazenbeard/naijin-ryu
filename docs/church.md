# Naijin-Ryu, Church of Ascension

**Source:** `Naijin-Ryu, Church of Ascension – Foundational Document Package.docx`, headings 1–8.

This page indexes a **proposed institution and its authored doctrine**. The source's use of “official” and “legal” does not independently establish registration, tax exemption, or operation.

## Identity in the source

The package names **Naijin-Ryu, Church of Ascension** and discusses contemplated corporate wording **Naijin-Ryu Church of Ascension, Inc.** Its stated motto is **“Integrating the Shadow, Illuminating the Self.”**

The organization is described as universalist and voluntary. Members are not required to give up an existing belief system, interpret cosmological symbols literally, or engage in martial practice. Optional Naijin-Ryū movement is positioned as one potential somatic expression of broader teaching.

## Eight principal sections

1. **Formal Name and Identity:** names, purpose, universalist ethics.
2. **Core Doctrine Statement:** Pleroma, Divine Spark, Archons, Six Aeons, Shadow Walkers, interpretation.
3. **Governance and Leadership:** founder-led beginning, possible later distributed leadership and board oversight.
4. **Clergy and Ordination Model:** suggested responsibilities and emergent recognition through service and demonstrated care.
5. **Articles of Incorporation Language:** proposed Indiana nonprofit formation text.
6. **Bylaws Outline:** suggested organizational rules and accountability mechanisms.
7. **Archetype Integration Framework:** structured work with archetypal and disowned aspects of experience.
8. **Spiritual Practices Overview:** ritual, reflective and communal activities.

## Boundaries

The package sketches governance but does not prove that its incorporation text has been filed, its board constituted, or its requirements legally reviewed. Spiritual support is not the same as professional mental-health treatment. Teaching a martial art requires its own safeguards and competence.
