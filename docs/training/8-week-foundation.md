# Eight-week Fundamentals Activation: editorial teaching framework

**Status:** proposed standardization for organizing training material; no contact or advanced-force instruction. Based on `Phases of SURGE Training Manual.docx` Phase 1, `Phase 1 Fundamentals.pdf`, `Stances for Naijin-Ryū.pdf`, and `Naijin-Ryū Phase1 Mental Conditioning.docx`. The original source documents retain their own durations and directions.

**Format:** four practice opportunities a week with space for recovery; the environment pillar is a recurring awareness theme. Week numbers describe **minimum planning units**, not permission to advance without demonstrated safe skill.

| Week | Movement and learning focus | Reflective focus | Review evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Orientation, training agreements, neutral posture, exits, awareness | Guard Life, Not Ego | Can explain when **not** to engage |
| 2 | Balance and stance transitions, comfortable mobility | Centerline Ascension | Stable non-contact movement under feedback |
| 3 | Controlled timing and footwork across directions | Attention under distraction | No uncontrolled collisions or overreach |
| 4 | Falling-safety concepts with qualified supervision; review prior work | Shadow Integration | Recognizes limits and communicates stoppage |
| 5 | Four SURGE domains in isolation, instructor-selected low-risk practice | Skill as Sacrament | Accuracy and respect over speed |
| 6 | Gentle transitions between concepts; situational awareness | Responding rather than reacting | Retains balance and chooses disengagement |
| 7 | Consistent performance under safe feedback and varied conditions | Reflection without self-aggrandizement | Corrects technical errors without escalation |
| 8 | Consolidation, rest/recovery review, controlled demonstration | Service and humility | Instructor assessment of movement, judgment and safety |

## Ready-to-use introductory sessions

For a participant **resuming physical training**, the martial workstream offers a [first-week restart plan](../../programs/martial-training/phase-1-restart-week.md) and four [ready-to-run solo sessions](../../programs/martial-training/phase-1-restart-week.md), [weeks 1–8 progression gates](../../programs/martial-training/phase-1-progression-gates.md), with a [private log template](../../programs/martial-training/restart-session-log.md). These are **editorial adaptations** of this source-grounded framework, not a rewriting of the original Word/PDF manuals. Personal injury modifications require individual guidance; do not store a participant's history in this repository.

## Training day themes

**Day A — Shock (principle-level):** defensive readiness, movement organization, space and timing, controlled pad-oriented or noncontact study as selected by a qualified instructor.

**Day B — Unbalance:** center of gravity, movement, foot placement and safe adaptation to a shifting base.

**Day C — Reversal:** relaxed pivots, response to pressure in controlled non-injurious drills and maintaining composure when a planned action fails.

**Day D — Grapple:** ground orientation, safe exits, body-position literacy, consent and prompt stopping. No novice chokes, joint injury demonstrations or unsupervised throws.

**Environment** is present every day as recognition of exits, obstacles, surface hazards, bystanders and opportunities not to fight.

## Promotion gate

A learner is ready for consideration only when the instructor can document **all** of: safe participation; comprehension of four core ethics; balanced movement; reliable communication of boundaries and stoppage; appropriately supervised skill fundamentals; and awareness of when to disengage. Extra practice is not a failure. Do not claim that an eight-week participant can safely handle a real physical threat.

**Medical qualifier:** bodily condition, history of injuries, medications and recovery needs are individual matters handled privately with relevant professionals. Historical injury modifications appearing in founder-specific materials are not universal prescriptions.
