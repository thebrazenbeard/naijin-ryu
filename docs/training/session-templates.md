# Session structure and progression log

**Purpose:** a consistent reporting format usable across beginner, intermediate and instructor-level plans. It harmonizes the manuals without adopting a fixed medical or combat prescription.

## Suggested session structure (adaptable)

| Segment | Intent | Instructor decisions |
|---|---|---|
| Check-in | Consent, readiness, goals and changed limitations | Modify or stop the session when needed |
| Preparation | Gentle movement, focus and posture | Suitable duration and intensity |
| Theme | One or more SURGE pillar concepts | Which supervised activities are appropriate |
| Integration | Apply ideas in controlled, consent-based learning | Environment, partner skill and safety limits |
| Reflection | Note decisions, errors, adjustment and de-escalation | Private feedback rather than public personal records |
| Recovery | Cool down and plan rest | Individual needs, appropriate onward care |

**Time is not a success metric.** The source manuals contain 60-, 75-, 90- and 120-minute examples. In this editorial framework those are historical templates rather than mandatory session lengths. Participants can use shorter practice when appropriate. Two days of rest can be a legitimate part of an intensive week; no schedule can substitute for safe recovery.

## Sample learning record

```text
Date:
Curriculum track: standard / accelerated / extended / individual
Current phase and week:
Pillar(s) studied:
Safety and consent check completed:
Skills or concepts observed:
Instructor feedback:
Conflicts or concerns:
Reflection on Guard Life, Not Ego:
Next permitted progression (if any):
```

**Do not commit personal health notes, named student records, contact details, disciplinary reports or private clergy conversations to public GitHub.** Use a controlled private record system if an actual organization begins operating.

## Evidence ladder

1. **Scheduled:** an activity was planned.
2. **Attended:** participation was observed.
3. **Practiced:** an activity was performed under stated conditions.
4. **Assessed:** a qualified person reviewed behavior against stated criteria.
5. **Qualified:** an authorized decision maker explicitly recognized attainment.

None of these are equivalent merely because a calendar week ended. A philosophical stage or spiritual experience cannot replace a technical safety assessment.
